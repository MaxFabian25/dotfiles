# Authored prose

Use this reference for drafting, development, or craft work on English or Danish essays, talks,
or personal narratives. Its [Development](#development) section also applies to the argument of an
academic paper, report, or thesis. Follow the requested genre and [editing scope](editorial-practice.md).
When matching a supplied style sample, use the [rules for voice samples](editorial-practice.md#voice-samples).

## Missing author material

Work from the brief, draft, earlier answers, and relevant material already supplied. When a
passage needs the author's experience or judgement that these sources do not contain, ask a
focused question about that passage: the event that prompted it, what changed, the evidence for
a claim, or what the author now thinks and remains unsure about.

Start drafting when the available material supports the requested piece. Work on supported parts
while a necessary question remains open. Keep unresolved gaps visible as editorial queries in a
working draft and resolve them before presenting that text as finished. Synthesis and explanation
can draw their value from selecting and connecting existing material; personal experience and
novel opinions are needed only when the brief or claim depends on them.

## Development

Let each paragraph advance the piece by extending, testing, illustrating, or complicating what
came before. Use the [reasoning checks](reader-core.md#reasoning-checks) when an apparent connection
lacks support.

For an argument, address the strongest supported objection: answer it or narrow the claim.
When no substantive objection is supported by the available material, address any material
limitation. Develop the author's position from the supplied material. An explanation or narrative
can fulfil its purpose without adopting a debatable thesis.

For talks and speeches, write for listeners who cannot reread. Keep signposting and purposeful
repetition when they help listeners follow the argument, and unpack dense clauses for
comprehension on first hearing.

## Craft after editing

Compare the revision with the source where an image, joke, hesitation, recurring word, or change
of rhythm carries meaning. Restore choices that the edit weakened without solving a reader's
problem. Keep mixed feelings and deliberate repetition when they matter to the piece.

Strengthen a passage with supplied detail. Use [focused questions](#missing-author-material)
when a revision needs an experience or feeling that the author has not supplied.

Change a fragment, long sentence, or uneven paragraph only when it obstructs comprehension or
fails the requested style.
