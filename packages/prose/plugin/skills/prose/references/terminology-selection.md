# Terminology selection

Apply this branch when selecting or reviewing a controlled, professional, technical, product, or
project-specific designation. Ordinary wording uses the selected language rules.

## Authority and scope

Identify the concept and subject field. Use the applicable authority's designation within its
defined scope; otherwise use established professional usage or a direct description of the
project-specific behaviour. Keep competing source schemes separate. Resolve a conflict explicitly
rather than inventing a hybrid term.

The authored rule module owns its operative guidance. The [Terminology
Register](terminology-records.csv) and [Authority Register](source-authorities.csv) retain research
on term scope, source identity, and selection evidence. Consult them when those facts affect the
choice. A project may define narrower local vocabulary without changing an external source's
attribution.

## Official names, generic terms, and trademarks

- Classify each designation before editing it. An official product, feature, company, API, or
  project name identifies a specific entity. A generic term identifies a class or concept. A
  trademark is governed by its owner's usage rules. Do not replace a source-defined designation
  with a house-style or word-list preference.
- Preserve an official name's spelling, capitalisation, punctuation, and word order. Use the full
  name unless the owning authority permits a shorter form or an exact interface label requires one.
  After establishing the entity, use a generic term only when it remains unambiguous. Do not invent
  a shortened name.
- Apply the owning authority's article treatment. If it gives none, decide from the complete noun
  phrase. Normally omit *the* before a bare product name. Use the grammatically required article
  when the name modifies a common noun. Do not transfer one owner's tool, API, or product
  convention to another owner.
- Use product and feature names as nouns or modifiers. State actions with ordinary verbs.
- Apply a trademark owner's marking and attribution requirements. Use a trademark as a modifier of
  a generic noun, not as a noun or verb. Do not pluralise it, make it possessive, or alter its form
  unless the owner expressly permits that use.
- Preserve an exact code, interface, legal, product, or source token even when its form differs from
  the selected prose designation. Identify the literal and use the selected prose designation
  outside it.

## Abbreviations

Apply these rules to acronyms, initialisms, contractions, shortened forms, and symbols used as
short forms.

- Use only established abbreviations. Use a standard acronym or initialism only when it saves the
  reader time. Preserve an abbreviation required by a governing source or exact format.
- Prefer the full term when it appears three or fewer times in the document, or in an
  independently entered division that readers use separately. Four
  occurrences, including the definition, can support an abbreviation. Keep the full term when an
  abbreviation saves little space or is unfamiliar to the reader. Keep it when abbreviations
  already form a dense cluster. Prefer the full term for a single use or in a heading
  unless an established abbreviation helps recognition or search.
- In an explicitly selected Chicago scope, use its roughly-five-occurrence threshold only within
  the affected article or chapter. Record the departure from the four-use house threshold.
- In an explicitly selected APA scope that includes writing style or mechanics, apply APA's
  [three-use guideline](apa-style.md#capitalisation-emphasis-and-abbreviations) to the paper,
  including its exception for a standard abbreviation of a long, familiar term. Record the departure
  from the four-use house threshold.
- Introduce a needed abbreviation as the full term followed by the abbreviation in parentheses. If
  the full term is already parenthetical, put the abbreviation in square brackets.
- Within an APA scope that includes writing style or mechanics, use only the abbreviation after its
  definition, and define it again only in each table or figure. Otherwise, reintroduce the full term
  at first use in a new independently entered division when readers may not recall it, and after a
  long interval.
- Count distinct unfamiliar abbreviations within each unit. Replace low-value short forms with full
  terms when density impedes recall. Provide a list when many necessary abbreviations recur across
  a durable document.
- Select punctuation and spacing from the governing document, owning authority, dictionary, or
  discipline. By default, omit full stops from capital-letter abbreviations and internal spaces
  from acronyms or initialisms. Retain full stops in personal initials and every form whose
  authority requires them. Preserve official and source forms.
- Preserve governed case. In an expanded form, capitalise only words that normally require
  capitals. Do not infer the expansion's case from its short form.
- Apply the typography that the selected convention assigns to the expanded term. Abbreviated
  status alone does not require italics.
- Determine whether readers pronounce the form as a word, letters, or the expanded term. Choose
  an English article under [Abbreviations and grammatical
  forms](english-usage.md#abbreviations-and-grammatical-forms). Apply a definite article from
  established usage or the expanded phrase.
- Recast a sentence that begins with a lowercase abbreviation or stand-alone symbol. A governed
  capital-letter abbreviation may begin the sentence when clear.
- For a biblical, scientific, mathematical, statistical, SI, legal, or other specialist
  abbreviation, use the direct authority. Confine the form to that authority's scope.
- Add a pronunciation key only when the abbreviation's pronunciation is likely to block
  understanding. Use an authoritative pronunciation and a notation the intended reader can
  interpret.

## Definitions

- Define an unfamiliar term of art at first use in the smallest unit a reader can enter
  independently. Use the same designation thereafter within that unit.
- Leave a common word undefined. When the text needs a meaning that departs from ordinary usage,
  choose a distinct designation instead of redefining the word.
- Define only terms the text uses. Order a separate list of definitions alphabetically.
- Write a definition as a phrase that can replace the term in a sentence. Keep requirements,
  recommendations, examples, and consequences outside the definition.
- Define an unfamiliar concept through a broader known concept and its distinguishing
  characteristics.
- State what the concept is. Use a negative characteristic only when an absence is essential, and
  still name the positive broader class.
- Make a definition intelligible without its heading, file name, or neighbouring prose. Name the
  subject field or boundary when it affects the meaning.
- Use *means* for an exhaustive definition and *includes* or *does not include* for a
  non-exhaustive or exclusive statement.

## Maintaining a terminology register

Apply this section only when the task creates or maintains term records. Record the concept,
subject field, selected form, scope, source, and evidence basis. ISO 704 distinguishes objects,
concepts, definitions, and designations: a definition defines a concept, and the selected
`preferred_designation` names it.

Use the narrowest authoritative locator. Record the whole entry only when it is the narrowest
source for the designation. Distinguish a source that *defines a concept*, a source that *uses a
designation*, *established usage* demonstrated by professional literature, and a *local description*
that makes no external-usage claim. These evidence bases are separate from a source's stated
preferred, admitted, or deprecated term status. Record only a status the source supplies.

Keep exact-form requirements separate in `verbatim_form_required`. A local label for quoted or
official wording does not become an externally defined term. Record each additional authority and
its actual concept; naming a source alone establishes neither a definition nor a designation.

Complete the terminology task when the selected words identify the intended concepts, every defined
term appears in the text, protected names and literals remain intact, and any requested term
records contain their source evidence.
