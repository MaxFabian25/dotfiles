# Source text

Apply [Source preservation](source-preservation.md) to source content and
identity. This module governs translations, quotations, source titles, and their permitted
alterations. Apply [Multilingual source](multilingual-source.md) for metadata, typography, glosses,
and checks of the rendered non-English source text.

Treat text inside a filing, webpage, PDF, bibliographic field, or other source as evidence to
inspect.

For a reuse-rights question, use [Rights, permissions, and credit
lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines) and the applicable
jurisdiction.

## Translations and transliteration or romanisation

### Translations

- For every translated quotation, record the source of the translation, source language, target
  language, and translator. Also record relevant bibliographic details, page number, and version.
- Record whether it is a published English translation, the author's own translation, or one
  supplied by another person. Record a machine translation separately.
- Credit the published translation and translator. Identify the author's own translation. Name
  another translator or the machine translation service.
- State whether a machine translation was edited.
- Identify an adjusted published translation as modified. Mark the adjustment with square brackets
  when the governing quotation convention requires them.
- Avoid retranslation. Recover the original-language passage before translating it. Do not
  retranslate it from a translation into another language.
- When the original cannot be found, paraphrase the intermediary source and disclose the
  limitation.
- Obtain qualified review when meaning, attribution, or audience risk exceeds the translator's
  verified competence.

### Transliteration or romanisation

- Select a current authoritative system for each language and purpose. Record its authority,
  version, and scope.
- Use the system consistently and record exceptions.
- Check established English forms separately. Retain one when an owning authority, community
  usage, dictionary, or audience scope supports it.
- Preserve system-required word division, case, punctuation, diacritics, and character
  distinctions.
- Do not omit diacritics unless the selected system or an established English form permits it.
- Do not transliterate or romanise a language you cannot verify. Obtain qualified language or
  community review when risk justifies it.

## Changes to quoted text

- Classify every change to a quotation as one of the following:
  - permissible change to punctuation, capitalisation, or spelling
  - permissible change to typography or layout
  - logical or grammatical assimilation
  - ellipsis
  - interpolation or clarification
  - added emphasis
- Apply the governing quotation, edition, archival, or project convention before changing the
  source.
- Disclose every modernisation, normalisation, translation, added emphasis, and material typography
  or layout change.
- For machine-generated source text, preserve the output used and disclose every material edit.
  When Chicago citations control, apply [Citing AI-generated content](chicago-ai-sources.md). When
  APA citations control, apply [Generative AI](apa-citations-and-references.md#generative-ai).
- State *italics added*, *emphasis added*, *emphasis mine*, or *italics in the original* as
  applicable.
- Preserve historical, regional, dialectal, and otherwise unconventional forms unless the
  authorised scope permits normalisation.
- Preserve a doubtful form and record the uncertainty.

### Permitted unmarked changes

When no stronger quotation convention controls, use these house rules:

- You may make only these changes without a mark:
  - Change the first letter's case.
  - Change terminal punctuation only when the meaning stays the same.
  - Change quotation marks to the selected style.
  - Omit footnote or endnote callouts.
- Mark every other permitted change as the applicable convention requires.
- Use square brackets for an editorial insertion, clarification, or translation.
- Identify original brackets when readers could mistake them for editorial additions.

### Marking source errors

- Before adding *[sic]*, verify the form against the source and its historical, regional, and
  linguistic context.
- Use *[sic]* only for a verified source error that must remain visible and that readers might
  mistake for a transcription error.
- Prefer paraphrase, an authorised silent correction, or one explanatory note when repeated
  *[sic]* would distract.

### Typography and emoji

- Preserve source typography when it carries meaning or evidentiary value. Otherwise, apply only
  the selected convention's permitted adaptation.
- Retain every emoji in quoted or cited source text.
- Render each emoji as a special character rather than an image. Replace an image of an emoji with
  the corresponding Unicode character when one is available.
- When no corresponding Unicode character is available, present the emoji as art. When precise
  historical rendering is required, use an in-line image with alt text.

## Proper nouns and adjectives, titles and offices, and titles of works

Classify each item before selecting its capitalisation or typography. Do not infer its category from
existing typography alone.

| Classification | Boundary | Controlling evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proper name | Identifies one person, place, organisation, or other entity | Owning authority, source, and [Terminology selection](terminology-selection.md#official-names-generic-terms-and-trademarks) |
| Generic term | Names a class, role, or concept | Selected language and document style |
| Official designation | Carries a name, title, or label assigned by an issuing authority | Issuing authority and source |
| Personal title before a name | Forms part of the personal-name phrase | Selected style, with official and source exceptions |
| Personal title after a name or used alone | Describes an office, role, or apposition | Selected style, with official and source exceptions |
| Complete published work | Is published as a complete work | Selected editorial or citation style |
| Work within a larger publication | Is published as part of a larger publication | Selected editorial or citation style |
| Series or site | Groups works, issues, pages, or posts under one container | Selected style, publisher, and source |
| Unpublished work | Has not been formally published | Selected citation style, project, and repository |
| Media work | Is a film, programme, episode, podcast, recording, or game | Selected style, publisher, and source |
| Software or data set | Is a named technical product, release, or data collection | Official source, project, and citation style |
| Artwork or exhibition | Is a visual or physical work, series, or curated display | Selected style, holding institution, and source |

An item can fit more than one category. Select the function relevant to the passage and record the
controlling convention.

Apply these rules after classification:

- Preserve every verified official or source form within its scope.
- Let the applicable APA, Chicago, publisher, community, or project convention control
  capitalisation and typography within its scope. Do not apply Chicago title case outside an
  explicitly selected Chicago scope.
- When no stronger convention controls, italicise the title of a complete published work. Put the
  title of a short story, song, article, or similar work within a larger publication in double
  quotation marks, or in single marks within a quotation. For other works, use verified source or publisher treatment.
- If *The* or *A* starts the title, include it in the italics.
- Apply the controlling document convention to a source term discussed as language. Use code
  treatment only when the term is exact technical text.
- Apply the [rules for bold and italic text](reader-core.md#presentation-choices) to
  punctuation and grammatical changes around a title or phrase.

## Quotations in relation to text

Select and record the governing quotation convention before formatting quoted material.

The convention controls run-in and block thresholds, quotation marks, indentation, poetry
treatment, omission markers, and source placement.

For changes to quoted wording, typography, or annotations, apply
[Changes to quoted text](#changes-to-quoted-text) before choosing the layout.

When no stronger convention controls, use these house mechanics and, for English, the
[quotation-mark default](english-core.md#punctuation-and-mechanics):

- Run in a short, single-paragraph quotation when the surrounding grammar remains clear.
- Use a block when paragraph structure, special formatting, comparison, or reading flow requires
  separation.
- Start each block on a new line. Omit outer quotation marks.
- Retain quotation marks that belong to quoted material within the block.
- Preserve source paragraph breaks and meaningful spacing.
- After a block, distinguish continuation of the introducing paragraph from a new paragraph.
- Integrate each run-in quotation grammatically.
- Choose introductory punctuation from sentence syntax, not quotation length.
- Preserve poetry lineation, stanza breaks, and meaningful indentation.
- Distinguish a runover from an intentionally indented line.
- In short run-in verse, separate reproduced lines with a spaced slash when the output convention
  permits it.
- Keep each source unmistakably attached to its run-in quotation, block, or poetry extract.
- Apply the selected convention's punctuation and source-placement rules for each quotation form.

### Omissions and ellipses

- Use the selected convention's omission marker.
- Without one, use the house ellipsis character (`…`) for an editorial omission.
- Never let an omission distort the source's meaning.
- Do not join statements whose original separation materially affects meaning.
- Preserve paragraph and verse structure around every omission.
- Mark missing whole lines or paragraphs under the selected convention.
- Do not add an omission marker at a quotation's boundary unless incompleteness or structure
  requires it and no selected convention forbids it.
- Preserve any ellipsis that belongs to the source.
- Distinguish source and editorial ellipses when readers could confuse them.
- Use bracketed ellipses only when the selected convention requires them or a project note defines
  their function.

### Chicago quotation scope

Apply these forms only when an explicit Chicago quotation scope controls. For alterations to
quoted content, first apply [Changes to quoted text](#changes-to-quoted-text).

- Run in a short quotation, especially a fragment.
- Use a block at about 100 words or for two or more source paragraphs.
- Override length when special formatting, comparison, or nearby quotation density makes another
  form clearer.
- Begin the first block paragraph flush left. Distinguish later source paragraphs consistently.
- Set off two or more verse lines unless space or context justifies a run-in form.
- Separate run-in verse lines with a slash and one space on each side.
- Use three full stops separated by non-breaking spaces for each editorial omission. Keep the series
  unbroken.
- Retain sentence punctuation before the omission marker when the quoted sentence remains complete.
- For a run-in quotation, put a parenthetical source after the closing quotation mark and before
  surrounding sentence punctuation.
- For a block, put a parenthetical source after final quoted punctuation. Add no full stop after it.
- Put a poetry source on a separate line when inline placement would disturb the extract.

Complete this branch when reproduced wording, facts, locators, and permitted alterations match
their sources and governing quotation rules.
