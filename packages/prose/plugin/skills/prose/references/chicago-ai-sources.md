# Citing AI-generated content

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation includes content that the author relied on and that a
chatbot or similar AI tool generated. This includes quoted and paraphrased content.

## Disclose tool use and cite generated content

- Make clear how the tool has been used in the text, preface, acknowledgements, methods, or another
  visible location.
- State what the tool contributed and how the author checked or changed it.
- Cite each specific contribution, whether quoted or paraphrased, where it occurs, in addition to
  the general statement of how the tool was used.
- Acknowledge in the text or note when cited AI-generated text has been edited or adapted.

## Source record

Record the fields below before rendering the citation:

| Required fields | Other source elements and conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Publisher or developer, tool, date the content was generated, and relevant prompt or prompt summary | Available version number, publicly archived copy, retained copy, specific locator, acknowledgement of editing or adaptation, and access restrictions |

Give the prompt verbatim when concise and necessary. Otherwise, provide a faithful prompt summary.
Summarise multiple prompts when a conversation is too long to reproduce.

## Prompts, AI-generated text, and publicly archived copies

- Preserve the prompt and AI-generated content on which the author relied.
- Record their sequence when conversation context affects the cited response.
- Record the version number when the interface or retained copy supplies it.
- Use the date the content was generated, not the later date when the conversation was opened.
- Prefer a publicly archived copy when disclosure is safe and governing policy permits it.
- Otherwise, retain a copy of the prompt and AI-generated content when privacy, law, policy, and
  security permit it.
- Record where the publicly archived or retained copy is stored and any access restriction.
- Do not publish a prompt or AI-generated content that exposes confidential or personal
  information.

## Citation forms

Treat the tool as the content's author for citation purposes. Record its publisher or developer
separately.

| Citation form | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Notes and bibliography | Cite the content in text or a full note. Use the verified source record, including its prompt or prompt summary, available version number, locator, editing or adaptation acknowledgement, and applicable publicly archived copy. Omit it from the bibliography by default. |
| Shortened note | Give the tool, a distinctive prompt, and a useful locator. |
| Author-date | Cite specific content where it occurs. Connect the citation to the statement about how the tool was used. Also connect it to the retained copy. Omit a reference-list entry by default. |
| Bibliography or reference-list exception | Include an entry only when governing policy requires it or repeated use makes it useful. Cite it under the publisher or developer rather than the tool, and include a publicly available URL. |

Apply the selected Chicago system's punctuation, date position, short-title, and locator rules to
AI-generated content.

## Authority, permission, and reuse rights

- Treat AI-generated content as a primary source only for claims about that content.
- Verify each substantive claim against the authority or original source that governs it.
- Do not cite AI-generated content as authority when an authoritative source supports the claim.
- Do not infer permission, licence, ownership, or reuse rights from generation or public access.
- Apply [Rights, permissions, and credit
  lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines) separately to copied or
  adapted material.

Complete this branch when each relied-on AI output records its source, disclosure, prompt scope,
date, and selected Chicago treatment.
