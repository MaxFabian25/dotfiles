# Websites, blogs, social media, interviews, and personal communications

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns a website, blog, social-media item, interview,
or personal communication.

Classify the source before choosing fields. For private content, apply [Private
sources](#private-sources). Record applicable permission limits. Do not turn a private exchange
into a public source by citing it.

## Source types

| Source type | Required source elements | Other source elements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web page | Page title or description, site title or description, owner or sponsor, publication or update date, and URL | Revision or access date, time stamp, original URL, archive date and URL, and whether content was modified or deleted or the site discontinued |
| Website as a whole | Site title or description, owner or sponsor, publication or update date, and URL | Revision or access date, frequently updated time stamp, permanent, publicly available archive, and relevant site section |
| Blog post or content posted to an electronic mailing list or forum | Author of the blog post or name of the correspondent, post, subject, or thread title, blog, list, or forum title, date, and URL | Larger publication title or host site, *blog*, *list*, or *forum* when needed, wording on the site for commenter identity, reply date, and archive |
| Social media content | Name and handle, enough text from the original post to identify it, platform, date, and URL | Time and zone, series of photos or other identifying description, fact that the post was deleted, permanent, publicly available archive, and retained copy |
| Interview | Names of the person interviewed and interviewer, place or date (or both), and where any available transcript or recording may be found | Brief identifying information, publication or posting date, platform, time stamp, and permission to quote |
| Personal communication | Name or description appropriate in context, conversation, message, or other communication, recipient, and date | Relevant medium, confidentiality agreement, retained copy, and permission from the owner to include an address |

## Private sources

Treat private posts, comments, list or forum messages, and direct messages as personal
communications. Do the same for a conversation, letter, email, text, or private recording. Never
supply a URL for content that is not publicly available.

## Citation forms

| Source | Notes and bibliography | Author-date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Web page or site | Prefer text or a full note. Put the page title first in a note. If a bibliography entry is required, begin with the owner or sponsor. | Put the year after the owner or sponsor. When only an access date exists, use *n.d.* as the year and retain the access date in the entry. |
| Blog, forum, or public social media post | Prefer text or a note. Begin a blog bibliography entry with the author of the post. Begin an electronic mailing list or forum entry with the correspondent. Social media posts, comments, and replies normally remain in text or notes. | A social media reference list entry may support repeated text citations. Cite comments and replies in text through the original post when possible. |
| Unpublished interview | Prefer text or a note. Include it in a bibliography only occasionally or when governing requirements call for an entry. | Usually cite in text as an unpublished interview or personal communication. Use a reference list entry only when governing requirements call for one. |
| Published or broadcast interview | Treat it like an article or other item in a periodical. Cite it under the interviewee rather than the interviewer. For an interview consulted online, include the URL or database name and other relevant information. | Put the publication or posting year after the interviewee. Use the corresponding periodical, web, or multimedia reference list entry. |
| Personal communication | Cite in text or a note. Do not add a bibliography entry by default. | Cite in text with the person, *personal communication* or a more specific term, and the date when needed. Do not add a reference list entry by default. |

Choose the fields for the source type, then apply the selected system's name order, date position,
punctuation, short-title, and locator rules.

## Web pages and websites

- Set a website title in roman type without quotation marks. Italicise it only when it shares the
  identity of a work normally set in italics.
- Put a titled page or section in quotation marks. A generic page description may remain in roman
  type without quotation marks.
- Record the page and site separately. Also record the owner or sponsor when it differs from the
  site title.
- Prefer the publication date or date of the most recent update or revision. Use an access date
  only when no publication, update, or revision date can be found or governing rules require it.
- Add the displayed time and zone when a frequently updated page needs that precision.
- Record the URL. For a page likely to change or become unavailable, retain a permitted copy.
  Record a permanent, publicly available archive when one exists.
- When citing an archive, record its archive date and URL. Also record the original URL when the
  archive link does not expose it.
- If the page changed, disappeared, or was deleted, state that condition. When a permanent,
  publicly available archive exists, cite the archived version used.
- In author-date, when only an access date exists, use *n.d.* under [Undated and forthcoming
  works](chicago-source-forms.md#undated-and-forthcoming-works).

## Blog posts, electronic mailing lists and forums, and social media content

- For a blog post, record the author of the post, quoted post title, italic blog title, date, and
  URL. Add the larger publication title and the word *blog* when needed for identification.
- For content posted to an electronic mailing list or forum, record the correspondent. Preserve the
  original wording of the subject or thread. Add the list or forum, host site, date, and URL.
- For a public social media post, record the name, handle, platform, date, and URL.
  Add the time and zone when they identify the cited post.
- Preserve the platform name used when the content was posted. Do not silently rename a historical
  platform to its current brand.
- Quote only enough post text to identify the source and support the use. Treat quoted wording,
  spelling, punctuation, symbols, and emoji as [source text](source-text.md).
- Describe a series of photos, video, repost, reply, or thread when that information affects
  identification or meaning.
- Cite a comment or reply to the relevant original post. Use the wording on the site for the
  commenter's identity and record the date.
- Public posts usually remain in text or notes. Add a bibliography or reference list entry only when
  repeated citation or governing policy makes it useful.
- Check whether the same information is available in a published source. Prefer that source when it
  better serves the reader.
- Retain a permitted copy because a post can change or disappear. Mark a deleted post and cite a
  permanent, publicly available archive when one exists.
- For private content, apply [Private sources](#private-sources).

## Interviews

- For an unpublished interview, identify the person interviewed and the interviewer. Add brief
  identifying information, if appropriate, and the place or date of the interview (or both).
- Record where any available transcript or recording may be found. Preserve any collection title,
  institution name, interview number, or other locator needed to find it.
- Record the time stamp for a cited passage in a recording when it is useful to another reader.
- If the interviewee remains unnamed, use brief identifying information appropriate in context.
  Explain any confidentiality agreement or decision to withhold the name in the work's method or
  source note.
- Do not reveal an identity withheld under a confidentiality agreement.
- Cite an interview that has been published or broadcast or made available online under the
  interviewee. Record the interviewer. Then apply the rules for the periodical, website, or
  recording consulted.
- Distinguish the interview date from its publication, broadcast, or posting date. Record both when
  the distinction matters.
- Verify permission before quoting unpublished interview material when permission is required.

## Personal communications

- Identify the correspondent unless a confidentiality agreement requires withholding the name.
  Otherwise, use a description appropriate in context and explain why the name is withheld.
- Name the communication as a conversation, interview, letter, email, text, direct message, or
  other relevant form. Mention the medium only when it helps identify or assess the source.
- Record the date. Identify the recipient when it is not clear from the surrounding prose.
- Omit email addresses, account identifiers, telephone numbers, private links, and comparable
  addresses. Include one only when needed in a specific context and with its owner's permission.
- Do not use *et al.* to hide people in an author-date personal-communication citation.
  Identify each cited person elsewhere in the text.
- Rarely place a personal communication in a bibliography or reference list. A private exchange
  does not become available to readers merely because the author kept a copy.
- Retain cited messages, recordings, or transcripts only when law, policy, security requirements,
  and any confidentiality agreement allow it. Record any permission to identify or quote.
- A retained copy supports verification. It does not grant permission to publish private content.

Complete this branch when each source records its applicable creator, title, date, container,
locator, access facts, and Chicago form.
