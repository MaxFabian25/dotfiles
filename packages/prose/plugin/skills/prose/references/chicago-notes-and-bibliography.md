# Chicago notes and bibliography

Apply this branch when the selected Chicago citation system uses notes and a bibliography.

Use the Chicago forms in [Chicago source forms](chicago-source-forms.md). Record the note location
and bibliography policy before drafting.

## Note numbers

- Begin each article's or chapter's note series with 1.
- Number notes continuously through a whole book only when it has no chapters or other internal
  divisions.
- Use superscript markers in text.
- In published notes, use full-size numbers followed by full stops.
- Accept the host's superscript note numbers in manuscripts.
- Put each marker after the applicable sentence or clause.
- Place it after punctuation except when it precedes a dash.
- Normally place it after a closing parenthesis.
- Put it inside only when the note applies solely to parenthetical material.
- Do not put a marker in a chapter title.
- Put chapter-wide material in an unnumbered note, not a title marker.
- Use one marker for all citations and commentary at the same location.
- Never reuse a note number out of sequence.
- Repeat the material under a new number or use a verified cross-reference.

## Shortened citations

- With a full bibliography covering every cited work, a shortened note may appear at first mention.
- With no bibliography or only a selected bibliography, give a full note at first mention.
- When no bibliography exists, prefer a full note at its first appearance in each chapter.
- After full details exist, use surname, distinctive short title, and locator.
- In successive notes to the same source, an author-only or title-only short form with the locator
  may replace the fuller short form. Prefer the fuller form unless the text already names the
  author or title.
- Add given names or initials only to distinguish identical surnames.
- Usually keep a title of four words or fewer in full.
- Prefer shortened notes to *ibid.*
- Do not use *idem*, *op. cit.*, or *loc. cit.* in ordinary Chicago notes.
- Before shortening a non-English title, verify that omitted words do not govern retained words.
- Get language-qualified review when that dependency is uncertain.

## Commentary and quotations in notes

- When a note supports main-text material and adds commentary, put the source first.
- Separate that citation from commentary with a full stop.
- For a quotation inside a note, put its source after the quotation's terminal punctuation.
- Do not wrap the whole source in parentheses.

## Several citations in one note

- Group citations under one marker when they share a location.
- Separate grouped citations with semicolons.
- Order grouped citations by the material they support.
- Make every citation-to-claim relationship unambiguous.

## Footnotes versus endnotes

- Follow the publisher's required note location.
- When choice remains, prefer footnotes for immediate lookup and concise notes.
- Prefer endnotes for lengthy commentary or layouts that cannot carry dense notes.
- Keep footnotes short enough to fit their pages.
- Group back-of-book endnotes by chapter and place them before the bibliography.
- Put notes after each chapter when chapters have different authors or circulate separately.
- Give endnotes clear chapter labels and navigation.
- In electronic output, use linked notes when fixed note locations do not apply.
- Use the host's note function for automatic renumbering.

## Bibliographies

- Record whether the bibliography is full, selected, or omitted.
- Record whether it is a single list or segmented.
- Omit the bibliography only when the notes supply complete publication details.
- Prefer a full bibliography for a book-length work.
- Include every substantially used and cited work in a full bibliography.
- Exclude personal communications and works merely mentioned but not relied on.
- Add uncited consulted works only when they are especially relevant.
- Label a selected bibliography and explain its selection principle in a headnote.
- Segment only when meaningful categories make sources easier to find.
- Introduce each segment with an explanatory subheading.
- List each source in only one segment.
- Use word-by-word alphabetisation unless governing instructions select letter-by-letter.
- For repeated authorship, alphabetise works by title and ignore initial *A*, *An*, or *The*.

## Author's name and title of work

- In a full or shortened note, name one or two authors; for more than two, give the first followed
  by *et al.* In a work without a bibliography, give the first full note the [bibliography author
  list](chicago-source-forms.md#citation-forms).
- Do not invert a name that already uses family-name-first order.
- Verify uncertain name order with the author or an authoritative source.
- Preserve the source's name form unless a verified identification adjustment is needed.
- Preserve source spelling, hyphenation, punctuation, number forms, and diacritics except for
  verified Chicago citation adjustments.
- Use sentence case for non-English titles under the source language's capitalisation rules.
- Do not alter unfamiliar capitalisation without language-qualified review.
- Retain an initial non-English definite article because it may govern following inflection.
- When adding an English translation, place it in brackets and use sentence case.

Complete this branch when note markers and placement follow the selected note policy, shortened
citations identify the full source, and bibliography entries follow the recorded coverage, name,
and title rules.
