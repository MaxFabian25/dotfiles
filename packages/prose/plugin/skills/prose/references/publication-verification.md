# Publication verification

Use the section that matches the work: final-format publication, a research archive or citation
library, rights clearance, LIX reporting, or reader testing. Apply publication administration when
preparing that deliverable, not merely because a short passage could later be published.

## Final-format publication

Before publishing or exporting a maintained document, apply the
[final manuscript check](editorial-verification.md#final-manuscript-handoff) to one complete output.
Inspect other formats for unique content and format-specific failures. Check the title,
description, abstract, contributors, dates, version, language, rights, accessibility, and keywords
that the output actually exposes.

Keep enough evidence to reproduce the relevant checks:

| Field | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Output | Format, version or date, renderer or build, and approved source version |
| Proofreading | Full comparison in one format and unique-content checks in other formats |
| Electronic testing | Reading order, headings, navigation, links, characters, displays, layout, and reflow |
| Accessibility | Applicable tool and version, reviewer perspective, governing standard, result, and unresolved barriers |
| Corrections | Material correction, exception, responsible person, and result after rechecking |

When the task includes accessibility conformance or material barriers to the intended readers, use
applicable tools and human review. Review alternatives, captions, table meaning, keyboard use,
reflow, and contrast as applicable. For an accessibility conformance claim, name the standard, its
version, and the success criterion or clause, such as WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.1 or EN 301 549
V4.1.1 clause 10.1.4.1. When the source does not give the version, ask for it instead of supplying
one. Report unperformed checks instead of claiming conformance from proofreading.

Use ordinary host history when an earlier version cannot affect a reader's decision. When readers
can discover and rely on an earlier version, keep this publication record:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Version of record | Identifier, status, version, date, and description of changes |
| Earlier versions | Identifiers, availability, and notice displayed with each version |
| Corrections and retractions | Affected version, nature, date, reason, status, and reciprocal links |
| Reader notification | Affected recipients, risk, action, date, outcome, and responsible person |
| Supplemental content | Relation, identifier, availability, accessibility, and online-only status |

Notify affected recipients when an earlier version could mislead or harm them. Complete the
publication review when final formats agree with the approved source, material corrections have
been checked, and required notices and unresolved checks have an owner.

## Research records and citation libraries

Apply this section when maintaining a research archive, acquiring citation-library records, or
converting live citations to plain text. Ordinary citation formatting uses [Links, references, and
citations](references-and-citations.md).

For an archive, record the exact source version, revision date, and consulted format when they can
change content or locators. Record access dates for mutable or undated sources. Keep a lawful copy
of mutable, informally published material as a printout, screen capture, or permanent public
archive, with its location, capture date, and source URL. An archive copy does not confer reuse
rights.

For a citation library:

1. Check imported names, titles, dates, roles, containers, identifiers, and locators against the
   direct source; correct missing, duplicate, and misfiled fields.
2. Generate citations and check them against the selected style and source. Fix the source record
   or style settings, regenerate, and check the manuscript output.
3. Before converting live fields to plain text, back up the library and manuscript. Verify the
   converted output before using it as the final copy.

Complete the record operation when its requested archive or library identifies the actual source
and the resulting citations retain their verified fields and locators.

## Rights and permissions records

Apply this section when the task requests rights clearance or a permissions database or handoff.
Follow [Rights, permissions, and credit lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines)
for the actual reuse basis, scope, attribution, and notices. For each copied or adapted item in
scope, collect the relevant fields below; mark missing evidence and unresolved rights explicitly.

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Item and source | Direct item identifier, exact source, stable locator, source role and scope, edition or revision, and access date |
| Copyright owner or grantor | Name, contact information, and rights-database locator |
| Permission or other basis | Written permission, licence, public domain, fair-use assessment under governing law, or unresolved status |
| Scope of rights | Formats or media, territory, language and translation, duration, edition, quantity, distribution, and cover or promotional use |
| Reproduction or adaptation | Reproduction, adaptation, and derivative-work rights, reuse status, and exact modifications |
| Credit line and notices | Exact required wording and placement, source, attribution, notices, and modification statement |
| Releases | Model or person release and its permitted scope, when applicable |
| Third-party material | Separate copyright owner, permission or other basis, scope of rights, and restrictions for each included item |
| Verification | Verification date, evidence locator, verifier, expiry or renewal date, and unresolved restrictions |

Verify the applicable licence or permission at its direct source. Resolve a missing grant or an
uncertain scope with the owner, grantor, or applicable legal authority before the affected reuse.
Record a legal assessment only when a qualified authority supplies it for the governing
jurisdiction. Keep required credit wording and placement exact, including third-party material
inside the item. Complete clearance only for the uses the evidence actually permits.

## LIX reporting

Limit claims about LIX to statistical readability, rather than general text quality or understanding.

When a Danish document reports a LIX value, state what it claims to show and a repeatable counting
method, including every choice about what is counted. Give the formula as the average number of
words per sentence plus the percentage of words with seven or more letters. Report both components
and their sum. Include a numerical range or target only with its relevant source publisher and
reader group.

## Reader testing

Apply this section when reader testing is requested or required by the governing workflow.
The intended audience is the people the communication addresses and is expected to reach.
*Plain science writing* addresses scientific subjects for readers with varying education,
backgrounds, and interests; use the [plain-language source
evidence](source-evidence/plain-language.md#plain-language) within that scope.

State the questions, passages, and reader outcomes the test will assess. Choose representative
readers and the actual reading task. Record material knowledge, numeracy,
or format-comprehension barriers and separate observations from interpretations. Use *paraphrase
testing* for one-to-one testing of how a person interprets a passage. Use *usability testing* for
whether readers can find and understand information in the content as a whole.

Check the four plain-language outcomes: readers get the information they need (*relevant*), locate
it (*findable*), understand it (*understandable*), and use it (*usable*). Record what they could do
without help and what blocked them. Limit a resulting claim to the readers, material, and tasks
actually tested.

### Danish reader testing

For Danish prose, also apply these requirements:

- Choose bounded text types where errors have serious consequences. Record the selection and its
  limitations.
- Let each reader finish or abandon the task before concluding the observation. Ask neutral
  clarifying questions after a stop.
- Ask a concrete comprehension question and an experience question. Record both answers.
- Compare the original and revised passages against the stated questions and expected reader
  outcomes. Complete testing when each question has an observed result or a recorded evidence gap.
  Limit each resulting recommendation to the readers, passages, and tasks tested.
