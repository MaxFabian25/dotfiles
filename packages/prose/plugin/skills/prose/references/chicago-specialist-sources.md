# Special types of references

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns a specialist source type, such as a reference
work, database, patent, standard, artwork, sacred work, or classical work.

This branch selects authority and metadata. It does not reproduce a discipline's citation system.

## Select the governing authority

- Classify the source before selecting its citation form.
- Identify the authority governing its field, jurisdiction, repository, tradition, or organisation.
- Record the authority, edition or version, locator, and scope before drafting.
- Use that authority for specialist abbreviations, numbering, identifiers, version treatment, and
  source order.
- Use a repository's suggested citation only as metadata evidence. Verify it against the governing
  authority.
- When no specialist authority can be verified, use only the selected Chicago citation system.
- Mark the specialist form unverified. Do not claim specialist conformity.

## Source types

| Source type | Required source elements | Governing authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reference work | Resource and entry titles, author of a signed entry, edition, publication date or most recent update or revision, access date when needed, and URL | The selected Chicago system under [Reference works and databases](#reference-works-and-databases) |
| Database | Institution, database name, record title or description, access date, accession number, and URL | Repository guidance and the applicable data-citation authority |
| Dataset | Author or institution, displayed title, type of source, version, repository, publication date or years to which data apply, and DOI URL or other URL | Repository guidance and the applicable data-citation authority |
| Patent | Names of creators, description, year of filing, patent number, issue date, jurisdiction, and URL | Official patent office and applicable jurisdictional authority |
| Standard | Organisation, title, designation, edition, editor when named, approval or reaffirmation date, cited clause, and URL | Standards organisation and the authority governing the cited field |
| Work of art | Artist, title or description, date of creation or completion, medium, location of the work, museum accession number, and URL | Museum or other location, governing art discipline, caption rules, and reuse-rights rules |
| Bible or other sacred work | Work, book, chapter, verse, or other numbered section, cited version or translator, and edition details when relevant | Authority accepted by the cited tradition, discipline, publisher, or project |
| Classical work | Author, work, identifying numbers for books, sections, or lines, and cited edition, editor, or translator when relevant | Current authority accepted by the applicable classical, medieval, literary, or project field |
| Citation taken from a secondary source | Original source and locator, secondary source and locator, and reason the original is unavailable | Selected citation system applied to the secondary source and transparent identification of the original source |

The table identifies the required record. The governing authority controls the rendered form.

## Reference works and databases

- Distinguish a whole reference work from a signed entry.
- In notes and bibliography, normally cite a well-known dictionary or encyclopaedia in a note under
  the resource title rather than in the bibliography. List another reference work with full publication details, like a book.
- For an alphabetically arranged work, cite the entry in quotation marks instead of a volume or
  page. Add *under* before it when that aids clarity. Do not use *s.v.*
- Cite a substantial signed entry by its author, like a contribution to a multiauthor book. Such a
  citation may appear in the bibliography.
- Record the entry title exactly as the source gives it.
- For a continually updated resource, record its publication date or most recent update or revision.
  Use an access date when no publication, update, or revision date can be determined.
- Preserve the source's name order inside an entry title.
- Record an accession number when one is supplied. For online data, record a DOI URL or other URL.
- Distinguish publication dates, access dates, and the years to which the data apply.
- Cite a database as a whole for many related datasets only when governing policy permits it.
- List a dataset with its own DOI individually when citation tracking requires it.
- Apply [Quantitative prose](quantitative-prose.md) when prose interprets the
  data.

## Artwork and illustrations, patents and standards

- Verify a patent's creators, jurisdiction, patent number, year of filing, and issue date independently.
- Use the official patent register or office as the source of identity.
- Verify a standard's designation, edition, amendments, status, and cited clause at the standards
  organisation.
- Do not let a title replace a standard's designation or edition.
- Distinguish a citation for a work of art from its caption, credit, alternative text, and rights
  record.
- Apply [Visual and media prose](visual-and-media-prose.md) when a work of art or map is
  displayed.
- Apply [Rights, permissions, and credit
  lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines) before copying or adapting the
  work.

## Scriptural references, classical Greek and Latin references, and classic English poems and plays

- Select the authority and cited tradition, version, edition, or translation before abbreviating.
- Use book, chapter, verse, section, line, or other identifying numbers when the governing authority
  defines them.
- Do not substitute page numbers for those identifying numbers unless modern editorial content
  requires pages.
- Preserve the cited source's numbering and letter case when the governing authority requires them.
- Do not invent or mix abbreviations from different traditions, disciplines, or editions.
- Cite modern introductions, notes, translations, and commentary through their published source
  type.

## Citations taken from secondary sources

- Seek the original source before citing it from a secondary source.
- Cite a source from a secondary source only when the original remains unavailable.
- Record why the original could not be consulted.
- Identify the original source and the consulted secondary source where the citation appears.
- Cite and list the consulted secondary source under the selected system.
- In author-date, mention the original author and date in the text. Cite the secondary source in the
  reference list.
- State that the original was examined only when the available evidence establishes that fact.
- Preserve each locator's relationship to its own source.

Complete this branch when each specialist source follows its exact source type, authority,
required fields, locator, and governing citation form.
