# Legal and public documents

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns a legal or public document.

This module applies Chicago's recommendation to use a legal citation guide for legal and public
documents. It records verified metadata, defines no universal legal style, and provides no legal
advice.

## Consult the recommended stylebook or citation guide

- Classify the document and jurisdiction before selecting a citation form.
- Record the citation guide's name, edition or date, jurisdiction, document types, and project
  scope.
- Use the citation guide or system explicitly required by the court, publisher, institution, or
  project.
- For United States material, use one of Chicago's two recommended stylebooks. These are *The
  Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation* and the *ALWD Guide to Legal Citation*. Follow another
  requirement if it governs.
- For Canadian material, consult the *Canadian Guide to Uniform Legal Citation* or the applicable
  court requirement.
- For United Kingdom material, consult the applicable court, legislative, institutional, or project
  citation guide.
- For international material, apply the issuing body or tribunal's system before any project
  supplement.
- For another jurisdiction, use its official guidance or an accepted local citation guide.
- Do not default to a United States system merely because Chicago discusses it.
- Verify the selected stylebook's or citation guide's current edition before drafting.
- Stop rendering when the applicable citation guide, requirement, or jurisdiction remains
  unresolved.
- Do not present an unresolved form as conforming to a named legal system.

Chicago identifies the recommended stylebooks and the boundary for ordinary secondary works. The
applicable citation guide or requirement controls citation form, abbreviations, typefaces, ordering,
reporter priority, parallel citations, and short forms.

## Classify the legal or public source

| Source type | Required source elements |
| --- | --- |
| Case or court decision | Jurisdiction, court, case name, docket, decision date, neutral identifier, official reporter or series, parallel source when required, and procedural history. Also record the opening page and specific page or paragraph. |
| Constitution | Jurisdiction, instrument and version, adoption or revision date, official source, article, amendment, section, clause, and other subdivision |
| Statute, code, or ordinance | Jurisdiction, enactment title, public-law or chapter identity, code or compilation, edition or currency date, enactment date, section, and subdivision |
| Legislative material | Jurisdiction, issuing body and chamber, session or parliament, document type and title, bill, resolution, report, or document number, and date. Also record the speaker when relevant and a specific page or other locator. |
| Executive or regulatory document | Jurisdiction, office or agency, document type and title, order, proclamation, rule, notice, register, or code identity, issue date, effective date, and version. Also record a specific page or other locator. |
| Treaty or international agreement | Parties, title, signing or adoption date, entry-into-force status when relevant, official treaty series or identifier, article, page, and official source |
| Government archival document | Jurisdiction, creating body, title or description, date, repository, record group, series, file, item, folio, stable identifier, and access conditions |
| Intergovernmental document | Body and organ, document type and title, resolution or decision number, document symbol, session, date, official source, and article, paragraph, or page |

Record every available field specified by the applicable citation guide or requirement. Never
invent unavailable metadata.

Use an existing source-type branch for a freestanding report, book, or article. Do so when no legal
citation guide or requirement governs its citation form.

## Record the citation guide and version consulted

| Record | Include |
| --- | --- |
| Citation guide or requirement | Stylebook, guide, or system, edition or date, jurisdiction, document-type scope, and project requirement |
| Issuing body | Court, legislature, executive office, agency, archive, treaty body, or intergovernmental organ |
| Version consulted | Print source, online version authenticated by a government entity, official version, exact copy of the official version, or source consulted through a commercial database |
| Document version and currency | Decision or enactment version, amendments, consolidation, database currency, archive state, and historical or current scope |
| Document identifiers | Case and docket numbers, neutral identifier, reporter or code identity, document number or symbol, and repository identifiers |
| Dates | Decision, enactment, signing, adoption, issue, effective, publication, amendment, or access dates as applicable |
| Page number or other locator | Specific page, paragraph, section, article, clause, column, folio, attachment, record group, or other locator required by the citation guide |
| Online access | URL, commercial database, repository, archive, and any applicable identification number |

Record each field once. Let the applicable citation guide or requirement determine which fields
appear and in what order.

## Choose the version consulted

- Prefer a print source when the applicable citation guide requires or recommends one.
- Treat an online version authenticated by a government entity as print when the citation guide
  permits it. Apply the same treatment to an official version or exact copy of the official version.
- Record which of these descriptions applies to the version consulted.
- Do not treat a URL or government domain alone as proof of authentication.
- Distinguish an official reporter, gazette, code, or archive from a commercial reproduction.
- For a commercial database, record its name and any applicable identification number. For
  constitutions and statutes, also record database currency.
- Preserve a supplied neutral citation or official document symbol exactly.
- Distinguish a slip opinion from a reported decision.
- Distinguish a bill or proposal from an enacted law.
- Cite the enacted law for its legal effect unless the claim concerns legislative history.
- Distinguish session law, codified text, historical consolidation, and current consolidated text.
- Distinguish a draft, signed, ratified, and in-force treaty when that status matters.
- Distinguish daily, provisional, and permanent legislative records when their contents or locators
  differ.
- Verify amendments, repeal, supersession, and currency when the claim depends on current law.
- When no official version is available, identify the version consulted accurately.
- Do not describe a copy as an official version or exact copy without verification.

## Keep document identity separate from the page number or other locator

- Record the identifier that establishes the document or proceeding before a specific page or other
  locator.
- Keep a reporter's opening page separate from the specific page cited.
- Keep a code, article, section, or document symbol separate from its cited subdivision.
- Use the locator type required by the applicable citation guide or requirement.
- Make every page number or other locator match the consulted version and its pagination or
  numbering system.
- Record parallel or neutral citations only when the applicable citation guide requires or permits
  them.
- Preserve court, reporter, code, docket, and document abbreviations from the applicable citation
  guide.
- Do not invent a legal abbreviation from a Chicago example.
- Test official URLs and database identifiers without replacing legal identity with a link.
- For archival public records, apply [Papers, contracts, reports, and manuscript
  collections](chicago-unpublished-and-archival-sources.md) without replacing this module.

## Render under the selected system

- Apply the citation guide's sequence, punctuation, typefaces, and short forms.
- Apply its rules for notes, textual citations, bibliographies, and reference lists.
- Do not mix Bluebook, ALWD, Canadian, UK, international, or local forms within one governed scope.
- Record any project departure and the higher requirement that controls it.
- Use ordinary Chicago source-type rules for secondary scholarship when governing policy permits.
- Use the citation guide that governs the legal form. Apply Chicago to the surrounding work only
  within its recorded scope.
- Reclassify the source when its jurisdiction, document type, or cited version changes.
- Apply [Rights, permissions, and credit
  lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines) separately when reproducing
  protected material.

Complete this branch when each source records its jurisdiction, authority, hierarchy, locator,
date, and selected legal or public-document citation form.
