# English usage

Consult this reference for an English grammar or usage question, a grammar-focused copyedit, or a
specific unresolved grammatical choice. Apply only the sections relevant to that question.
[English core](english-core.md) owns house spelling and usage choices. The [authority and
preservation rules](../SKILL.md#authority-and-preservation) protect intentional dialect, literary
voice, and governed source forms during a grammar edit.

## Abbreviations and grammatical forms

Apply [Terminology selection](terminology-selection.md#abbreviations) when introducing an
abbreviation. For its grammatical form:

- Form an acronym's plural like an ordinary noun, without an apostrophe. Add a lowercase *s* only
  when the acronym represents a singular noun.
- For a numeral, word, or letter, add lowercase *s* when the result is clear. Recast an ambiguous
  form.
- Choose *a* or *an* from the first sound of an abbreviation as readers pronounce it, as in *an SEC
  filing* and *a NATO member*.
- Identify whether coordinated holders share one possessed thing or possess separate things. Mark
  only the final noun for joint possession and every holder for separate possession.
- Give each personal pronoun its possessive form.
- Decide whether a group noun expresses possession, participation, or attribution. Preserve an
  official form. Otherwise, follow the selected language authority.
- Use a trailing *-ing* clause only when its cause, result, or evidence relationship is explicit
  and supported.
- Make the logical actor of each infinitive, participial, or gerund clause clear. Rewrite an
  attachment that assigns its action to the wrong actor.
- Allow an adverb inside an infinitive when that placement best preserves meaning or natural flow.
- Place *only* beside the word or phrase it limits. Recast when another placement could change its
  scope.
- Keep verb tense consistent within a paragraph. Shift tense only when the time frame changes, and
  make the shift clear.
- In *one of those who* and *one of those that*, make the relative-clause verb agree with the plural
  antecedent.
- Distinguish referential pronouns from dummy *it* and *there*. Keep a dummy subject for clear
  expressions of weather, time, distance, or existence.

## Punctuation and mechanics

- Use the dictionary form for established compounds. Hyphenate an adjectival phrase before a noun
  only when the hyphen improves clarity.
- Omit a hyphen after *very* or an adverb ending in *-ly*. Normally omit it when the adjectival
  phrase follows the noun.
- Retain a suspended hyphen only for a coordinated hyphenated compound. Repeat the shared element
  for a closed compound or possible misreading.
- Hyphenate a prefix before a proper name, numeral, or date. Use a prefix hyphen to prevent ambiguity
  or mispronunciation.
- Use a hyphen in numbers that are spelt out.
- Put terminal punctuation inside a closing parenthesis only when the entire sentence is
  parenthetical. Otherwise, put it after the closing parenthesis.
- Put a comma after an introductory word or phrase.
- Put a comma before a coordinating conjunction that joins two independent clauses. Omit it before
  a shared predicate unless clarity requires it.
- Put a comma after a conjunctive adverb that opens a sentence. Start a new sentence before an
  independent restatement.
- Omit a comma before a restrictive clause introduced by *because*. Use one when the clause is
  nonrestrictive or omission could mislead.
- Close every comma-delimited interruption with a second comma when the sentence continues.
- Set off a name or other form of direct address with commas.
- Set off a nonessential phrase introduced by *such as* or *including*. Leave an essential phrase
  unmarked.
- Set off an explanatory restatement introduced by *that is*, *namely*, or *for example*. Use a new
  sentence for an independent clause.
- Use a comma for an omitted repeated word only when the remaining structure is clear. Restore the
  word when omission could mislead.
- Use a colon after a complete clause to introduce an explanation, example, or list. Keep a verb or
  preposition beside its completion.
- End a complete sentence with a full stop unless a direct question or permitted exclamation
  requires another mark.
- Begin a sentence with *And* or *But* when the word states the intended addition or contrast
  clearly.
- Retain a conjunction or clause-introducing *that* when omission could obscure a clause boundary or
  change coordination.
- Use *who* as a subject or subject complement. Use *whom* as an object when the document's register
  requires the distinction.
- Use *because* before a causal clause and *because of* or *owing to* before a causal noun phrase.
  Preserve contractual and reproduced wording.
- Use a plural noun when the number can vary. Use *one or more* when both singular and plural need
  explicit mention.

Check the changed sentence in context: its actor, time, emphasis, condition, and claim must retain
the intended meaning. A grammatical alternative does not authorise changing supplied voice or
source wording.
