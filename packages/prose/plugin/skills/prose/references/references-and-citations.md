# Links, references, and citations

Apply this branch to a pointer, link, citation, footnote, attribution, reference-list entry, or
source register that the task adds, changes, or reviews. Distinguish navigation, an internal
cross-reference, and evidence for a claim.

For a pointer that controls an agent's next action, name the trigger and the first action after
opening the target. When deeper guidance is needed and the `writing-for-agents` skill is available,
consult it for pointer design.

## Source identities and roles

Identify the actual source and the version or edition used. Preserve supplied author names,
source order, work identity, dates, and locators; let the selected citation style govern their
presentation. Preserve supplied exercise metadata. Do not invent missing facts.

An authority is responsible for the attributed definition, designation, standard, or rule.
Supporting literature can support a local rule without defining that local treatment. A primary
source reports the original content at issue. Describe other roles directly. A repository revision
can identify a source version; the responsible publisher and work remain the authority for its
claims.

Cite a primary source after consulting it. If you used a secondary source, identify that source
and make the indirect attribution clear. Cite the source actually used and give a page or other
stable locator for a direct quotation. Check quoted wording against that source and apply
[source text](source-text.md). Paraphrase when exact wording is unnecessary.

## Citation placement and style

Put a citation beside the material claim it supports. A dense source or term register may use one
pointer that resolves each attribution to its authority and scope.

Use the citation system selected by the governing instruction. APA controls only its selected
scope; Chicago controls only its selected scope and system. Otherwise use the host or project
convention, or a concise bibliographic record preserving the facts. Discipline, document form,
footnotes, and parentheses alone do not select a citation system.

Verify dates and versions when they can change the content or locator. Use the publication date
rather than a generic modification timestamp; use a revision date when it functions as the
publication date or is the only date available. Follow the selected style's rule for access or
retrieval dates. Without a selected style, publish an access date when the source lacks a
publication or revision date.

## Link selection and behaviour

- Name the destination or purpose in link text that makes sense alone, not a generic label such as
  *here*, *click here*, *more*, or *link*. Use an exact title or short unique description and link
  to the narrowest relevant heading. A bare URL is appropriate when the URL itself is content.
- Keep useful local context. Link to the authoritative guide instead of recreating it. Repeat a
  link when a distant section or independent entry point needs it.
- Keep punctuation outside link text unless it belongs to the title or literal. Include an
  introduced abbreviation with its full form. Give a linked command its description when needed
  for recognition. Separate adjacent links with a character.
- Copy identifiers exactly, including case, encoding, query syntax, fragments, and trailing
  slashes. Prefer a DOI URL, persistent source link, or official identifying page. Avoid
  search-result, session, proxy, and account-specific URLs. Name a subscription database when its
  URL cannot identify the source outside the current session, unless the selected citation style
  omits database information for that source.
- Test new or changed links when the task permits access. Check that they reach the named source or
  identifying page; check gated destinations while signed out when possible. Report an unresolved
  destination rather than implying verification.
- Keep links in the current tab by default. Identify a required new window, download file type,
  email action, or on-page destination. Identify another domain when that distinction matters.

## Keeping track of your research

When the requested work includes maintaining a research archive, importing citation records, or
converting a citation library to plain text, read [Research records and citation
libraries](publication-verification.md#research-records-and-citation-libraries). These operations
need durable records and checks beyond formatting a citation.

## Footnotes

Keep required information in the main text. Use a footnote when the governing style permits it
and the main text would be less clear. Follow that style's markers and punctuation. When it gives
no rule, use an unbracketed superscript numeral: after the full stop for a whole-sentence note,
immediately after the word or clause for a narrower note, before a following full stop. Capitalise
and end footnote text with a full stop when it is a complete sentence.

## Rights, permissions, and credit lines

Respect the actual licence, permission, reuse scope, required credit, and notices for copied or
adapted material. Public availability, repository hosting, and attribution alone do not grant
permission. Verify the applicable basis when reuse needs it; never invent permission or imply
endorsement. Keep a legal assessment within the authority and jurisdiction that supplies it.

Credit reused material even when permission is unnecessary. Put a visual or table's credit beside
it, following the grantor's wording and placement. Use the selected citation style's credit wording
when it has one. Otherwise, use *reprinted* for an unchanged reproduction, *adapted* for a modified
item, and *data from* for a new display based on source data.

When the task requests rights clearance or a permissions record, read [Rights and permissions
records](publication-verification.md#rights-and-permissions-records). If requested reuse exceeds a
confirmed basis, identify the unresolved scope and obtain clearance from the grantor or applicable
authority. A link or independent paraphrase may meet a task that does not need exact reproduction.

Complete the requested reference work when identities, attribution, locators, selected style, and
actual reuse restrictions are correct. Report any check that the available sources cannot resolve.
