# APA Style

Select this branch only from an explicit user, publisher, journal, institution, or project
requirement for English, Danish, or multilingual prose or citations. A recipient, document type,
discipline, footnote, parenthetical citation, or mention of the manual supplies no selection authority.

Before applying an APA rule, record the selecting authority and exact scope. The scope can include
editorial mechanics, paper format and organisation, headings, scholarly-writing style, quotation
treatment, citations and references, tables and figures, or a stated combination. Apply only the
sections within that scope. When the requirement selects APA Style without a narrower scope, apply
every relevant section to the prose unit. An instructor's or institution's requirement overrides
APA Style within its stated scope.

This module is an independently worded implementation of authority `AUTH-APA-7` in [source
provenance](source-manifest.md), checked against the manual's text. It identifies the skill's
APA-specific house departures. APA guidance published after the manual comes from
`AUTH-APA-STYLE-WEB`.

Apply English spelling, word choice, grammar, sentence punctuation, and date conventions only to
authored English prose. For authored English, apply the [English house choices](english-core.md),
including British spelling, which departs from the manual's American dictionary standard (Section
6.11). Within the recorded APA scope, an APA rule below controls over an English house choice on
the same point, except where this module states a house departure.
For Danish prose, use the [Danish language and institutional adaptations](danish-core.md#apa-i-dansk-prosa).
Apply the shared APA organisation, heading, table, and figure rules within the selected scope. For
in-text citations, reference entries, and source forms, apply [APA citations and
references](apa-citations-and-references.md).

## APA conventions in force

Apply the sections below only within the recorded APA scope. Source text and governed tokens
keep the form required by the applicable source or interface. The stated house departures and
supplements are not attributed to APA Style.

### Numbers, dates, and mathematical notation

Apply this section when APA governs the numerical or mathematical category. Apply English
sentence, title, and date conventions only to authored English.

| Convention | Adopted form |
| --- | --- |
| Numbers | Use words for zero through nine and numerals for 10 and above, including ordinals (*third*, *10th*). Use numerals below 10 for a number before a unit of measurement, a statistical or mathematical function, a decimal or uncommon fraction, a percentage, a ratio, a percentile or quartile, a time, duration, date, or age, a score or point on a scale, an exact sum of money, and a numeral discussed as a numeral. Use words for a common fraction and a universally accepted phrase. For back-to-back numerical modifiers, write one as a word and one as a numeral (*ten 7-point scales*), and reword when the combination is hard to read. |
| Numbered series | Write a number that follows a noun and marks a place in a numbered series as a numeral, and capitalise the noun: *Table 2*, *Step 1*. Keep *p.*, *pp.*, and *para.* in lowercase. |
| Initial numbers | Do not begin a sentence, title, or heading with a numeral. Recast the text, or spell out the number when recasting would be worse. |
| Percentage and currency symbols | Use `%` or a currency symbol with a numeral, and repeat it in a range (`75%–80%`, `$10–$20`). Use the symbol without a numeral only in a table heading or a figure label or legend. Elsewhere, without a number, write *percentage* or the currency's name. After a number word that begins a sentence, write *per cent*; APA spells it *percent*, and *per cent* applies the British house spelling. |
| Unit of measurement abbreviations | In running prose, write out a unit of measurement that is not accompanied by a numeric value. Use its established abbreviation or symbol with a numeric value or in a table heading. Use an established unit abbreviation or symbol without defining it at first use. Do not abbreviate *day*, *week*, *month*, or *year*. Governed labels keep their required form. |
| Dates | Use numerals for the day and year. The manual sets no explicit day and month order for running text, but its comma rule and examples assume month, day, and year, with the year set off by commas (Section 6.3). It accepts the writer's national format only for the student title-page date (Section 2.3). The [house date form](number-forms.md#dates-and-times), `13 April 2026` without commas, departs from that running-text form. Reference-list dates follow the APA date element in [APA citations and references](apa-citations-and-references.md#reference-entries). |
| Statistical and mathematical type styles | Use standard (roman), bold, and italic type according to function. Put Greek letters, identifiers in subscripts or superscripts, and non-variable abbreviations in standard type. Use bold for vectors and matrices. Italicise other statistical symbols and algebraic variables. Keep SI unit symbols upright. |
| Displayed equations | Treat an equation as part of its sentence and punctuate it accordingly. Display a complex equation on its own line. Number an equation only when later text refers to it, and refer to it as *Equation 3*, never *Eq. 3*. |
| Division slash | In a mathematical expression, give each slash one unambiguous numerator and denominator, and use parentheses when it contains more than one division. In a compound unit, use at most one slash; otherwise use centred dots and negative exponents, with parentheses as needed (nmol·hr⁻¹·mg⁻¹). |

The selective equation-numbering rule departs from APA's rule to number all displayed equations.
Within this scope, APA's number rules replace the [counts default](number-forms.md#number-words-and-ordinals).
Apply the other [quantity and unit rules](quantitative-prose.md#quantities-and-units).

### English grammar and wording

Apply this section when the recorded APA scope includes English writing style or mechanics.

| Convention | Adopted form |
| --- | --- |
| First- versus third-person pronouns | When describing your research or expressing your views, use the first person rather than the third person. In a sole-authored paper, use *I*. In a coauthored paper, use *we*. Refer to people in general with a suitable noun instead of editorial *we*. As a house supplement, a document issued in an organisation's name may use an institutional voice; a sole- or coauthored paper may not. |
| Contractions and colloquialisms | In original prose within the selected APA writing-style scope, avoid contractions and colloquialisms. Retain source wording in a direct quotation or official title. Retain a contraction when it is the subject of a linguistic example or part of an idiom or common saying. |
| Data | Treat *data* as a plural noun: *these data are*. |
| Coordinating conjunctions used in pairs | Place the first conjunction immediately before the first part of the parallel construction. Use *between X and Y*, *both X and Y*, *either X or Y*, and *neither X nor Y*. Use *not only X but also Y*. Pair *both* with *and*. |
| Repetition of parallel elements | Include all elements of the parallel construction before and after the coordinating conjunction. Repeat an introductory word, such as *that*, before each parallel clause. When a clause has two parts, do not use a comma or semicolon before the conjunction. |

### Capitalisation, emphasis, and abbreviations

Apply this section when the recorded APA scope includes English writing style or mechanics.
Heading and display-title case belongs to [Paper organisation and
headings](#paper-organisation-and-headings) and [Tables and figures](#tables-and-figures).

| Convention | Adopted form |
| --- | --- |
| Abbreviations beginning a sentence | Do not begin a sentence with a lowercase abbreviation or a stand-alone symbol. Recast the sentence or write out the term. Begin with a symbol joined to a word only when recasting would make the sentence indirect or awkward. |
| Titles of works in the text | Write the title of a work mentioned in the text in APA title case. Italicise the title of a book, report, periodical, or other stand-alone work. Put the title of an article or chapter in double quotation marks. Write software and app names in roman type. Reference-list titles follow [APA citations and references](apa-citations-and-references.md#reference-entries). |
| Typographic emphasis | Do not use bold or italics merely to assert importance. Use bold for structural hierarchy. Use italics only where a convention requires them. Examples include italicised titles of works, symbols set in italic type, and a key term at its first defined use. |
| All capitals | As a house supplement, do not use all-capital words, headings, or sentences for emphasis. Keep capitals where an abbreviation, ticker symbol, status token, official mark, or other governed token requires them. |
| Theories, models, and procedures | Lowercase the name of a theory, concept, hypothesis, principle, model, or statistical procedure, such as the *capital asset pricing model*. Capitalise only a personal name within it, as in the *Gordon growth model* or the *Black–Scholes model*. Keep its abbreviation in capitals, such as `CAPM`. |
| Abbreviation use | As a guideline, abbreviate a term when the abbreviation is conventional, readers are likely to know it better than the full term, it saves considerable space, and it appears at least three times; a standard abbreviation for a long, familiar term may appear fewer times. Group-author abbreviations follow [APA citations and references](apa-citations-and-references.md#in-text-citations). Define an abbreviation at first use in the text under the [house definition form](terminology-selection.md#abbreviations), then use only the abbreviation. Do not define an abbreviation in a heading, and use one in a heading only after the text defines it or when the dictionary lists it as a word. Define it again in each table or figure that uses it. Leave undefined an abbreviation listed as a word in the dictionary, a unit or time abbreviation with a numeral, a Latin abbreviation, a statistical symbol or abbreviation that represents a statistic (*M*, *SD*, *t*, *F*, *df*, *p*, *N*, *n*, *OR*, *r*), and a Greek-letter symbol used with its meaning in Manual Table 6.5. Define other statistical abbreviations, such as ANOVA, CI, and SEM, and a Greek-letter symbol used with another meaning, such as a model parameter. |
| Latin abbreviations | Keep *cf.*, *e.g.*, *etc.*, *i.e.*, *viz.*, and *vs.* in parenthetical material only. Use their English equivalents in running prose. Keep *et al.* in narrative and parenthetical [citations](apa-citations-and-references.md#in-text-citations). |
| Abbreviation plurals | Add lowercase *s* without an apostrophe. Keep a unit symbol unchanged in the plural. |
| Abbreviation full stops | Write `U.S.` and `U.K.` with full stops only as adjectives, as in *U.S. dollars*. Writing *United States* and *United Kingdom* in full, which APA permits, is the house form for a noun. Do not use full stops in other capital-letter abbreviations or acronyms, such as `APA` and `IQ`. Keep full stops in personal initials, Latin abbreviations, and reference abbreviations such as *p.* and *ed.* Official names and verbatim source text keep their source form. |

The typographic-emphasis rule narrows options that APA Style permits.

### Sentence punctuation

Apply this section when the recorded APA scope includes English writing style or mechanics.

| Convention | Adopted form |
| --- | --- |
| Serial comma | Before the final conjunction in a series of three or more |
| Spacing after punctuation marks | In ordinary prose, use no space before a full stop, comma, colon, or semicolon and one space after it. Use one space after sentence-ending punctuation. Do not put a space after an internal full stop in an abbreviation (*U.S.*, *e.g.*) or around the colon in a ratio (*1:8*). Mathematical notation, URLs, source code, and LaTeX-command syntax keep their required spacing. |
| Semicolon | Use between closely related independent clauses or list items that already contain commas. Use a full stop when clauses carry separate ideas. |
| Independent clauses | Separate two independent clauses with a full stop, a semicolon, or a comma plus a coordinating conjunction. |
| Independent clauses with a coordinating conjunction | Put a comma before a coordinating conjunction that joins two independent clauses. Do not put a comma between the two parts of a compound predicate; recast the sentence when it needs a break for clarity. |
| Introductory comma | Put a comma after an introductory clause or phrase. After a short introductory phrase the comma is optional; keep it when leaving it out could cause a misreading. |
| Nonessential or nonrestrictive elements | Comma-bracket a nonessential or nonrestrictive clause, phrase, or appositive. Leave information that identifies or restricts the noun unbracketed. |
| Coordinate adjectives | Put a comma between adjectives that independently modify the same noun and can be reversed or joined by *and*. Write cumulative adjectives without a comma. |
| Conjunctive adverb | Between independent clauses, put a semicolon before *however*, *therefore*, *nevertheless*, or another conjunctive adverb, and put a comma after it. A full stop may replace the semicolon. |
| Ampersand | Write *and* in prose. Keep `&` only in an official name, an established abbreviation, APA citation output, or a citation in a table or figure. |
| Slash | In running prose, keep the slash for a numerator and denominator (*X/Y*), a unit with a numeric value, an established term, or APA citation output, such as a republished work's dates (*1920/2013*). Write a ratio with a colon (*1:8*). Otherwise write *and*, *or*, or *versus*. |
| Possessive apostrophes | Add apostrophe-*s* to a singular noun or proper name, including one that ends in *s*. Add an apostrophe alone to a plural noun that ends in *s*. Add apostrophe-*s* to a plural noun that does not end in *s*. Form an ordinary plural without an apostrophe. |
| Question mark | Use for a direct question. Use a full stop for an indirect question or a sentence that only describes a question. |
| Exclamation mark | In original prose within the selected APA writing-style scope, reserve exclamation marks for verbatim quotations and official titles. |
| Adjacent parentheses | Do not place two prose parenthetical groups back to back. Recast when possible, or combine them in one set separated by a semicolon. |
| Parenthesis punctuation | Put the full stop inside the closing parenthesis only when the entire sentence is parenthetical. Otherwise put sentence punctuation after the closing parenthesis. |
| Em dash | Do not use an em dash in original prose. Recast the sentence with a sentence break, a comma pair, parentheses, or a colon. |
| En dash | Spans, ranges, and paired relationships (`--` in LaTeX) |
| Quotation marks | Use double marks for an ordinary quotation. Use them also for a word or phrase referred to as itself, and at first use only for an ironic, slang, or coined expression or a newly introduced label. Use italics instead for a key term being defined. Do not use quotation marks for emphasis, hedging, or ordinary technical terms. Put full stops and commas inside the closing mark, except when exact governed text requires external punctuation or a citation follows the quotation at the end of a sentence. Put colons and semicolons outside. Put a question mark or exclamation mark inside only when it belongs to the quoted material. Otherwise put it outside. |

The em dash and slash rules narrow options that APA Style permits. The coordinate-adjective,
question-mark, and exclamation-mark rows, the plural-possessive sentences, and the semicolon row's
separate-ideas sentence are house supplements; the manual leaves general grammar to other writing
guides. APA Manual Sections 4.8, 4.16–4.24, 6.1–6.17, 6.22–6.29, and 6.32–6.47 supply the mechanics
basis.

## Paper organisation and headings

Apply this section when the recorded APA scope includes paper format, organisation, or headings.
It owns APA heading case and format.

Use the paper type and required elements that the recipient or institution specifies. Without a
more specific requirement, identify the title, author or approved identifier, affiliation, and
other required front matter. Give the body a hierarchy that follows the argument. Without another
requirement, order the paper as title page, abstract, text, references, footnotes, tables, figures,
and appendices, and start each of these on a new page. Footnotes may instead stand at the foot of the
page, and tables and figures may instead follow the paragraph that first calls them out.

Do not head the introduction *Introduction*. The paper title at the top of the first page of text
acts as its Level 1 heading, and headings within the introduction start at Level 2. Use only the
heading levels the paper needs, in order from Level 1. Start each main section at Level 1, give
sections of equal importance the same level, and give a divided section two or more subsections.
Do not number or letter headings unless the institution requires it.

| Level | Format |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Centred, bold, title case. The text begins as a new paragraph. |
| 2 | Flush left, bold, title case. The text begins as a new paragraph. |
| 3 | Flush left, bold italic, title case. The text begins as a new paragraph. |
| 4 | Indented, bold, title case, ending with a full stop. The text continues on the same line. |
| 5 | Indented, bold italic, title case, ending with a full stop. The text continues on the same line. |

Centre and bold a section label, such as *Abstract* or *References*. Label one appendix *Appendix*.
Label several appendices with capital letters in the order of their first mention in the text, such
as *Appendix A* and *Appendix B*, and call out each appendix in the text by its label. Put an
appendix title on the line after its label, centred, bold, and in title case.

Number footnotes consecutively with superscript Arabic numerals. Place each callout after any
punctuation mark except a dash, which it precedes, with no space before it, and inside a closing
parenthesis when it applies only to the parenthetical matter. Do not put a callout in a heading.
Refer to an earlier note as *see Footnote 3*.

In APA title case, capitalise the first word of a title, heading, or subtitle and the first word
after a colon, em dash, or end punctuation. Capitalise every noun, verb (including *is*, *are*, and
*be*), adjective, adverb, and pronoun, every word of four or more letters, and the second part of a
hyphenated major word (*Self-Report*). Elsewhere, lowercase articles and conjunctions or
prepositions of three letters or fewer.

APA Manual Sections 2.11–2.17, 2.26–2.28, and 6.17 supply the organisation, heading, and
capitalisation basis.

## Quotations

Apply [Quotations in relation to text](source-text.md#quotations-in-relation-to-text). Put a
quotation of fewer than 40 words inside double quotation marks and change the source's double marks
within it to single marks. Present 40 words or more as a block indented 0.5 in., without quotation
marks, using double marks for any material the source sets in quotation marks, even when the
source uses single marks.

Introduce the quotation in grammatical prose and give its citation and locator under [APA
citations and references](apa-citations-and-references.md#in-text-citations). Place a parenthetical
citation after a short quotation or at the end of the sentence, and put the sentence's full stop
after the citation's closing parenthesis. After a block quotation, place the citation after the
final punctuation, with no full stop after it. For a source known only through another work, apply
[Secondary sources](apa-citations-and-references.md#secondary-sources).

Mark an omission within a quotation with three spaced full stops or the ellipsis character, with a
space before and after it, and use four full stops for a break between sentences. Do not put an
ellipsis at the start or end of a quotation unless the source has one. Show added emphasis in italics
and insert `[emphasis added]`, in roman type, immediately after the italicised words. Keep citations that appear
inside quoted text, and give those works reference entries only when you also cite them directly.

When a quotation exceeds the copyright holder's limit for use without permission, obtain any
permission needed and give a copyright attribution in a footnote to the quoted text instead of the
author–date citation. APA Manual Sections 8.25–8.35 and 12.14–12.18 supply the quotation basis.

## Tables and figures

Apply this section when the recorded APA scope includes tables and figures.

Treat any display other than a table as a figure. Number tables and figures separately, with Arabic
numerals, in the order of first mention. Refer to each by number before it appears, such as *Table 2
shows*, and not by its position or page. Number a display in a text appendix with the appendix
letter, such as *Table A1*, using *A* in a sole appendix. When an appendix consists of one table or
one figure and no text, its appendix label and title replace the display's number and title. Number displays that
form an appendix without text within it, such as *Tables D1 and D2*.

Put the label and number, such as *Table 1*, in bold above the display. Put the title one line
below it in italic APA [title case](#paper-organisation-and-headings), without a full stop. Align
the number, title, display, and notes flush left.

Give every table column a heading, including the stub column. Centre the column headings and write
them, the stub and cell entries, and the notes in sentence case. Left-align stub entries. APA centres
other cell entries, left-aligns long text, and permits numerical values to be aligned on the
decimal. For a column of numbers, the house [numeric table alignment](tables-and-figures.md) rule
replaces the centring. Carry comparable values to the same number of decimal places. Leave a cell
blank when data are not applicable, explaining it in a note when needed. Insert a dash when data
were not obtained or not reported, and explain the dash in the general note.

Use horizontal rules at the top and bottom and below the column headings, and optionally above a row
of totals. Use no vertical rules and no borders around cells. Avoid shading, and explain any
necessary shading in the general note. The house [merged-cells rule](tables-and-figures.md)
excludes the column spanners, and so the decked heads, that APA permits. Repeat the shared words in
each column heading, group rows under a label in the stub, or split the table into separately
numbered tables, not *Table 1A* and *Table 1B*. Repeat the heading row when a table continues on
another page. In a figure, write axis labels and legend text in title case and state units of
measurement, such as in the axis title.

Put notes below the display. Begin a general note with *Note.* in italics, followed by the note text
in plain type. Use it for the display as a whole. Give explanations, the meaning of emphasis, and
data sources first, then definitions of non-standard abbreviations and symbols, and end with any
copyright attribution. Mark a specific note with a superscript lowercase letter on the heading or
entry and at the start of the note, assigning letters from left to right and top to bottom. Use a
probability note for significance markers. Start each type of note on its own line, in the order
general, specific, probability.

Put a unit that applies to one column or row in its heading or stub entry, where a symbol form such
as *$ millions* may stand without a numeral. State a unit that applies to the whole table in words in
the general note, such as *Amounts are in millions of U.S. dollars*. The manual places units in headings
and stub entries and gives the general note to information about the whole table; the general-note
placement of a table-wide unit extends those rules.

Cite data that you reconfigure or reanalyse to produce different numbers with ordinary author–date
citations and locators in a note or source column, and list the sources in the reference list. When
a table reproduces another source's data unchanged, end its general note with a copyright
attribution that begins *Data are from*. As a house adaptation, when a table combines reproduced values with values you
derive, give the attribution for the reproduced values and cite the sources of the derived values in
the same general note. When you reproduce or adapt another work's display, end the
general note with a copyright attribution that begins *From* or *Adapted from*. Give the source
information in one of these forms, and then its copyright status:

- Article: `"Title of Article," by A. A. Author and B. B. Author, Year, Journal Name, Volume(Issue),
  p. x (https://doi.org/...)`, with the journal name and volume in italics.
- Book or report: `Title of Book or Report (p. x), by A. A. Author and B. B. Author, Year, Publisher
  (DOI or URL)`, with the title in italics.
- Chapter in an edited book: `"Title of Chapter," by A. A. Author, in E. E. Editor (Ed.), Title of
  Book (edition or volume, p. x), Year, Publisher (DOI or URL)`, with the book title in italics. Use
  *(Eds.)* and join two editors with *and*.
- Webpage: `Title of Webpage, by A. A. Author, Year, Site Name (URL)`, with the title in italics.
  Omit the site name when it repeats the author.

State the copyright status as *Copyright Year by Holder.*, a Creative Commons licence abbreviation,
or *In the public domain.* When permission was sought and obtained, end with the holder's required
wording or *Reprinted with permission.* or *Adapted with permission.* The attribution replaces an
in-text citation for the display. Give the source a reference entry as well, and check whether
reuse needs permission.

Make each display understandable without the text. Use the text to interpret a display and its key
values instead of repeating its contents. Present the same data in one format unless another format
serves a reader purpose. Pair colour with another carrier of meaning. APA Manual Sections 2.14 and
7.1–7.36 supply the table, figure, and accessibility basis, and Sections 12.14–12.18 supply the
copyright attribution basis.

## Bias-free language

Apply [Inclusive language](inclusive-language.md) to every mention of a personal or demographic
characteristic. APA Manual Chapter 5 supplies the bias-free language basis.

## Unlisted conventions

For an unlisted convention, consult the *Publication Manual of the American Psychological
Association* (7th ed.) when an authorised copy is available. Use the APA Style website for
supplemental content that the manual refers to and for guidance published after it. Where they
differ, follow the manual, except on a topic the manual does not address, such as generative AI,
and record the conflict. Otherwise, preserve the existing treatment and record the convention as
unverified. Claim conformity only to the selected rules.
