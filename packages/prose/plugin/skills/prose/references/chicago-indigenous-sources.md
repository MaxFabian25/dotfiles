# Indigenous sources

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns information from Indigenous peoples,
communities, Elders, or Knowledge Keepers.

Chicago distinguishes secondary sources related to Indigenous peoples, cultures, and histories
from information acquired directly from an Indigenous person. Information acquired from an
Indigenous Elder or other Indigenous Knowledge Keeper has additional requirements.

## Confirm suitability for publication, additional protocol, and additional permission

- Respect the authority of the Traditional Knowledge and Oral Traditions passed down through
  Indigenous Knowledge Keepers.
- Give this authority the same respect given to scholarly sources outside Indigenous communities.
- Confirm whether the information to be discussed and cited is suitable for publication.
- Confirm whether publication requires following additional protocol or obtaining additional
  permission.
- Record who supplied each confirmation, its scope and date, and any review or expiry condition.
- Do not infer permission from access, earlier publication, silence, academic custom, or a previous
  interaction.
- Do not publish the information while its suitability, additional protocol, or additional
  permission remains unresolved.
- Whenever possible, confirm these matters with the Elder or Knowledge Keeper.
- Do not substitute editorial judgement when the required confirmation is unavailable.
- Follow applicable Indigenous customary law and every confirmed additional protocol.
- Protect restricted information in drafts, fixtures, prompts, logs, and retained records.
- Retain evidence of confirmation only within the applicable access, storage, and retention limits.

## Classify and verify the source

Classify information acquired directly from an Indigenous person separately from a secondary
source. A secondary source relates to Indigenous peoples, cultures, or histories. Do not select
this branch merely because a creator is Indigenous.

- Information acquired directly from an Indigenous person can often be cited as a personal
  communication.
- Apply the additional requirements in this branch when the source is an Indigenous Elder or other
  Indigenous Knowledge Keeper.
- Confirm the name of the Elder or Knowledge Keeper and the nation or community to which they
  belong.
- Confirm a brief description of the information or teaching shared and the date of the
  interaction.
- Confirm a treaty territory and where they live before including either one.
- Confirm whether the Elder or Knowledge Keeper prefers a Traditional Name rather than a legal
  name. Also confirm whether a legal name should be listed.
- Cite a secondary source according to its source type, but use caution, especially with older
  materials.
- Check whether a secondary source ignored Indigenous customary law or used Indigenous information
  inappropriately.
- Acknowledge an oral source in the citation rather than crediting only the secondary source.
- Do not treat public availability as evidence of permission to republish knowledge.
- Record what has been confirmed and what remains unknown.
- An unknown status does not establish permission.

## Record information confirmed with the Elder or Knowledge Keeper

| Citation information | Record when applicable and confirmed |
| --- | --- |
| Elder or Knowledge Keeper | Name, preferred order and spelling, and whether a Traditional Name or legal name should be listed |
| Nation or community | The nation or community to which the Elder or Knowledge Keeper belongs |
| Information or teaching | A brief description of the information or teaching shared |
| Date of interaction | Month, day, and year of the interaction |
| Treaty territory | Treaty territory, if applicable |
| Where they live | Where the Elder or Knowledge Keeper lives, if applicable |
| Topic or subject of communication | The topic or subject when it is not mentioned in the text |
| Interview | Specify an interview only when it is not clear from the text |
| Suitable for publication | Whether the information to be discussed and cited is suitable for publication |
| Additional protocol and permission | Additional protocol to follow and additional permission to obtain before publication |

Record only information that the Elder, Knowledge Keeper, or applicable additional protocol permits
the project to retain.

## Follow additional protocol and obtain additional permission

- Confirm whether the information may be discussed, cited, quoted, paraphrased, summarised,
  translated, or described.
- Confirm medium, audience, access level, duration, and reuse when an additional protocol
  distinguishes them.
- Follow every additional protocol and obtain every additional permission before publication.
- Apply each confirmed limit within its recorded scope.
- If citation is permitted but disclosure is not, publish allowed attribution and omit restricted
  content.
- If permission changes before publication, revise or withdraw the affected material.
- Do not broaden permission for one use into permission for another.
- Apply [Rights, permissions, and credit
  lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines) separately for copyright or
  licence questions.
- Copyright permission does not replace any additional permission required to publish the
  information. Additional permission does not establish copyright status.

## Cite the confirmed information

- Build each citation only from information confirmed for publication.
- Use the confirmed name form and order. Do not add a legal name when it should not be listed.
- Preserve confirmed spelling, diacritics, capitalisation, and ordering.
- Include the confirmed nation or community.
- Include a brief description of the information or teaching shared and the date of interaction.
- Include a treaty territory or where they live only when applicable and confirmed.
- State that the communication was an interview only when that fact is unclear from the text.
- A personal-communication citation does not reduce the authority of the Traditional Knowledge and
  Oral Traditions passed down through an Elder or Knowledge Keeper.
- Apply the selected Chicago citation system only after the information has been confirmed.
- Chicago controls only the remaining order and punctuation that no confirmation overrides.
- Record every departure from generic Chicago and the confirmation that controls it.
- Apply [Websites, blogs, social media, interviews, and personal
  communications](chicago-web-and-personal-sources.md) to the communication form without replacing
  these requirements.
- Apply [source text](source-text.md) to exact wording and [Multilingual
  source](multilingual-source.md) to applicable language, transliteration, or romanisation
  decisions.
- Apply ordinary source-type rules to a secondary source without erasing acknowledged oral sources.
- Do not imply that a secondary source proves permission or speaks for the originating community.
- Include a bibliography or reference-list entry only when the selected system and permission
  allow it.

Complete this branch when each source meets the requirements for its source type. For information
acquired directly from an Indigenous Elder or other Indigenous Knowledge Keeper, confirm its
suitability for publication, naming, knowledge-holder role, and every required additional protocol
and permission before applying the remaining Chicago form. For a secondary source, preserve
acknowledged oral sources and complete the checks on customary law, permission, and attribution.
