# Chicago source forms

Apply this branch when Chicago governs source citation forms. Use the citation system recorded in
[Chicago-style source citations](chicago-scope-and-systems.md#chicago-style-source-citations).

Classify each source before rendering it. Gather the source metadata once. Then render each
citation in the selected system. Before converting a citation-manager export, check the source
itself and every cited field.

## Source records

Use the most specific matching row in the selected source-specific module. Record each field the
source supplies. Render a field when the row marks it as required or the source-specific rules
call for it. Never invent a missing field.

## Citation forms

Keep the same verified source identity across all forms. Change order, punctuation, and locator
coverage only as the selected citation form requires.

Apply Chicago title case to English cited titles. Under the 18th edition, capitalise prepositions
with five or more letters; see [the edition changes, section 8.160](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/help-tools/what-s-new.html).
When only an English translation of a cited title is given, identify the original language.

Across reference lists and bibliographies, preserve the source's author sequence, keep every
author after the first in normal order, and repeat author names in consecutive entries. List up to
six authors; for more than six, list the first three followed by *et al.*
Use neither 2-em nor 3-em dash substitution.

| Citation form | Author, editor, or organisation and date | Source details | Locator coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full note | Give personal names in normal order. For books, put publication facts in parentheses and join the main elements with commas. | Put a contribution or article title in quotation marks and its book or periodical title in italics. Use *in* for a book part, not for a periodical article. | Give the page, chapter, section, volume and page, time stamp, or other specific place that supports the note. |
| Shortened note | Give the lead surname or other lead element, a distinctive short title, and the locator. | Omit role abbreviations. Preserve the source title's word order and type treatment. | Repeat the applicable locator in every shortened note. |
| Bibliography | Invert only the first author's or editor's name. Put main elements in sentence-like groups separated by full stops. For books, place the year after the publisher. | Omit a specific page reference for a book or book part. Give a journal article's complete page range or article identifier. Magazine and news entries may be omitted when the governing bibliography policy permits. | Retain only locators that identify the source as a whole or that the source type specifically requires. |
| Author-date reference | Invert only the first author's or editor's name and put the year immediately after all names. | Keep the remaining source elements in their source-type order. Put the rest of a magazine or news date after its publication title. | Apply bibliography-like locator coverage, including full journal ranges but no ordinary specific page reference for a book. |
| Author-date text citation | Give the lead surname, organisation, or permitted publication-title substitute, followed by the year without intervening punctuation. | Omit editor, translator, compiler, and similar role labels. | Add a specific locator after a comma. Use a colon without spaces between a volume and page. |

## No-author and unlisted forms

When no author or editor is identified, do not add a name. For books and journal
articles, apply the title treatment for the selected citation form:

- In notes and bibliographies, normally begin with the title. Ignore an initial article when
  alphabetising the bibliography entry.
- In author-date reference lists, apply the title-first entry rule in [Author-date reference
  lists](chicago-author-date.md#author-date-reference-lists).
- For author-date text citations, apply the short-title treatment in [Multiple author-date text
  references](chicago-author-date.md#multiple-author-date-text-references).

For unsigned magazine and news articles, the publication title may supply the lead element. For
unsigned reviews, use the hosting periodical. Preserve explicit source attribution to *Anonymous*.

## Undated and forthcoming works

- When the publication date of a printed book or other fixed work cannot be found, use *n.d.* in
  place of the year. Substitute a bracketed estimated year, such as *[1750?]*, or add the estimate
  after *n.d.*, as in *n.d., ca. 1750*. For an online source or continually updated resource with
  only an access date, apply its source-specific module: notes and bibliography give the access
  date, and author-date uses *n.d.* as well.
- Use *forthcoming* in place of the year for a titled work under contract whose publication date is
  unknown. Cite a work not under contract as an [unpublished
  manuscript](chicago-unpublished-and-archival-sources.md#unpublished-manuscripts-and-private-contracts-wills-and-such).
- In author-date, keep *n.d.* lowercase and capitalise *Forthcoming* only in a reference list
  entry. In a text citation, put a comma before *n.d.* or *forthcoming* and keep a bracketed
  estimate in brackets.

## Locators

- Use Arabic numerals for numbered source divisions even when the source uses words or Roman
  numerals. Preserve Roman numerals for pages that are actually numbered that way, such as front
  matter.
- Give the first and last number in a closed range. Do not replace the last number with *f.* or
  *ff.*. Use *ff.* only when the end cannot be stated usefully, and use *passim* only for scattered
  references within a stated range.
- Omit *p.* and *pp.* when a number clearly denotes a book page. Add a label when another number
  would make the locator ambiguous.
- Label chapters, sections, books, notes, appendixes, figures, tables, maps, plates, lines, folios,
  or other nonpage locations clearly. Preserve recto and verso distinctions for numbered leaves.
- For a numbered note, attach `n` and its number to the page number; use `nn` for consecutive notes
  and `n` alone for a page's only or unnumbered note. For a note that spans two pages, put the page
  range before `n`. After a Roman-numbered page, put a space before `n`.
