# APA citations and references

Apply this branch when the recorded [APA scope](apa-style.md) includes citations and references.
Apply the shared [citation, link, and attribution rules](references-and-citations.md) and [source
preservation](source-preservation.md) where this branch gives no APA form. For Danish prose, use
the [Danish citation terms](danish-core.md#apa-i-dansk-prosa). Where the manual gives no form for a
source or case, this branch states a house adaptation, which is not attributed to APA Style.

## In-text citations

Use the author–date system. A parenthetical citation contains the author and year. A narrative
citation puts the author in the sentence and the year in parentheses beside the author's name; when
the year also appears in the sentence, omit the parentheses. Place each citation under [Citation
placement and style](references-and-citations.md#citation-placement-and-style).

- For a work with two authors, name both in every citation. Join them with `&` in a parenthetical
  citation and with *and* in a narrative citation. In a table or figure, use `&` in narrative
  citations too.
- For a work with three or more authors, give the first author followed by *et al.* from the first
  citation. When two works from the same year would shorten to the same form, write out as many
  names as needed to distinguish them and abbreviate the rest as *et al.* in every citation. When
  *et al.* would stand for only one name, write out every name in every citation, with `&` before
  the last in a parenthetical citation.
- When the first authors of different works share a surname but not initials, give the first
  author's initials in every citation, as in *(J. M. Taylor & Neimeyer, 2015; T. Taylor, 2014)*.
- Give only the year, even when the reference entry has a fuller date. Write *n.d.* for an undated
  work and *in press* for an accepted work that is not yet published.
- List several works in one parenthetical citation in alphabetical order, separated by semicolons.
  Give a shared author once, followed by the dates: *n.d.* first, dated works in date order, and
  *in press* last, as in *(Department of Veterans Affairs, n.d., 2017a, 2019)*. Treat works that
  shorten to the same first author and *et al.* as having a shared author, and list their years in
  date order. APA does not place an abbreviated group author; as a house adaptation, order it by
  its full name.
- Add a lowercase letter suffix, starting with *a*, to the year of works with the same author and
  year, as in *2018a* or *n.d.-a*, and use the same suffix in the reference entry.
- When a parenthetical citation ends a sentence, put the sentence's end punctuation after the
  closing parenthesis. After a run-in quotation, put the citation after the closing quotation mark
  and omit the quotation's own full stop.
- When other text shares the parentheses, put commas around the year when the text runs through the
  citation, as in *(see Koehler, 2016, for more detail)*, and put a semicolon between separate text
  and the citation, as in *(e.g., falsely balanced news coverage; Koehler, 2016)*. Do not nest
  parentheses.
- Do not use *ibid.* Repeat the citation. After the first narrative citation of a work in a
  paragraph, omit the year from later narrative citations of it in that paragraph, but keep the year
  in every parenthetical citation and whenever you cite more than one work by the same authors.
- For a work with no author, cite the title, shortened if it is long, in title case and the year, in italics when the
  title is italic in the reference entry and in double quotation marks otherwise. Use *Anonymous*
  only for a work signed that way.

Use a group author when the cover or title page names only the organisation. When it names
individuals, list them as authors and give the organisation in the source element. Use the most
specific responsible agency as the author and give a parent body as the publisher. Abbreviate a
group author only when the abbreviation is well known, avoids cumbersome repetition, or appears at
least three times. Define it once at the first citation, as in *(National Institute of Mental
Health [NIMH], 2020)* or *National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH, 2020)*, and then use the
abbreviation alone. Do not abbreviate two groups that would share an abbreviation.

Give a page number for a direct quotation from a paginated work, using *p.* for one page and *pp.*
for a range. When pages are absent, give a paragraph number as *para.*, a heading or section name,
or both, or a time stamp for audiovisual material. A paraphrase requires the author and year; add a
locator when it helps the reader find the passage in a long or complex work. Cite a section, letter,
note, table, figure, slide, or other part of a document through the whole document with a locator, as in *(National
Institute of Mental Health, 2019, Table 3)*.

## Secondary sources

Read and cite the original source when it can be consulted. Cite a work known only through another
source when the original is unavailable, out of print, or in a language you cannot read. Name the
original, with its year when known, and cite the source you read, as in *(Rabbitt, 1982, as cited in
Lyon et al., 2014)*. Omit an unknown year, as in *Allport's diary (as cited in Nicholson, 2003)*.
Give a reference entry only for the source you read.

## Personal communications

Cite an unrecoverable source, such as an email, interview, or unrecorded talk, in the text only.
Give the communicator's initials and surname, the words *personal communication*, and as exact a
date as possible in APA's form, as in *(T. Nguyen, personal communication, February 24, 2020)*;
this governed form replaces the house date form. Judge recoverability for the intended readers:
cite course, learning-platform, or intranet material they can open with a full reference. Cite a
recoverable version instead when one exists. Do not cite generative AI output as a personal
communication; apply [Generative AI](#generative-ai).

## Reference entries

Include a reference entry for each recoverable work cited in the text, and cite each listed work in
the text. Order entries alphabetically by the first word of the entry, letter by letter, so that
*Loft* precedes *Loftus*, and file a group author under its full name. Ignore an initial *A*, *An*,
or *The* only in a title that stands in the author position or that breaks a tie between works.
Place one-author entries before multiple-author entries with the same first author. Order
multiple-author entries that share a first author by the second author's surname, then the third,
whatever their dates. List works by the same author or authors by date: *n.d.* first, then dated
works from the earliest, then *in press*. Among works with the same author and year, put entries
with only a year first, then entries with fuller dates in date order, and order entries with
identical dates by title. Keep parts of a series in series order. Assign letter suffixes in that
order. Use a hanging indent in the rendered list.

Build each entry from four elements: author, date, title, and source. Preserve the source facts
while applying APA element order, punctuation, capitalisation, and type treatment. Rewrite a
citation that a source suggests into this form.

- **Author.** Invert each person's name and give initials. List up to 20 authors, with `&` before
  the last. For 21 or more, list the first 19, an ellipsis, and the last. Give a group author's full
  name as the source presents it, never its abbreviation, and join two group authors with `&` and no
  comma. As a house adaptation, when an organisation's works present its name differently, use the
  form on the most recent cited work in every entry and citation; the manual reproduces each work's
  form and gives no rule for variant group names. When a work has no author, move its title to the author position.
- **Date.** Give the year in parentheses. When the source type takes a fuller date, put the year
  first, as in *(2020, August 26)*. Use a last-updated date only when it clearly applies to the cited
  content, and ignore a last-reviewed date or a copyright year in a website's footer. For a
  generative AI tool, apply [Generative AI](#generative-ai). Write *(n.d.)* when no date
  applies and *(in press)* for an accepted work. For a republished work, give the year of the version
  you read, end the entry with *(Original work published 1920)*, and give both years in the text, as
  in *(Watson & Rayner, 1920/2013)*.
- **Title.** Use sentence case for the title of a work and of a part of a larger work. Italicise the
  title of a work that stands alone, such as a book, report, data set, or webpage. Leave the title of
  an article, chapter, or other part in roman type without quotation marks, and italicise the larger
  work in the source element: a periodical title in title case and an edited book title in sentence
  case. Put a report number or edition in parentheses after the title. After the title, add the
  bracketed description that the manual's example for the source type shows, such as
  `[Press release]`, `[Data set]`, or `[Presentation slides]`, capitalised and in roman type, and
  include one when in doubt. An article, chapter, book, report, or webpage takes none. For an untitled
  work, put the description in square brackets in place of the title. For a work in a language other
  than the paper's, keep the original title and add a translation of it in square brackets. Keep a
  title's own wording, including its date form; in a title or description you compose, use the
  [house date form](number-forms.md#dates-and-times).
- **Source.** Give the publisher without a business designation such as *Inc.* or *LLC*. Omit the
  publisher when it is also the author. Do not give a publisher location; give a place only for a
  work tied to one, such as a conference presentation.

Give a DOI for every work that has one, whichever version you consulted, and check for one before
treating a database article as a print work. Present a DOI as a `https://doi.org/` URL and give no
other URL with it. Otherwise give a URL that leads directly to the work when readers can use it. Do
not give a database name or URL for a work from an academic research database, such as EBSCOhost or
JSTOR, apart from JSTOR's primary-sources collection. Name a database only when it publishes
original proprietary works cited like articles, such as the *Cochrane Database of Systematic
Reviews*, giving its name in italic title case like a periodical, or holds
works of limited circulation, such as a dissertations database, giving its name in roman type like a
publisher. Give its home or login page when the work's URL is session-specific. Do not write
*Retrieved from* before a URL. Include a retrieval date, as in *Retrieved September 30, 2020, from*
followed by the URL, only for content designed to change over time and not archived. End a DOI or
URL without a full stop.

APA Manual Chapter 8 supplies the citation rules, Chapter 9 the reference elements and order, and
Chapter 10 the examples.

## Source forms

Classify a source by what it is, not by how you retrieved it. A report or press release found on a
website takes its own form; use the webpage form only when no other form fits.

## Generative AI

APA's guidance on generative AI appears on the APA Style website, not in the 2020 manual. Follow the
institution's policy on whether and how AI may be used.

- Do not cite AI output as a personal communication. Credit the company that developed the tool.
- Cite a tool as Company. (Year of the last update). *Tool or model name* [Large language model].
  URL of the tool. When the tool shows no update year, use the copyright year on its website or
  app. Use the bracketed description that best fits a tool that is not a chat tool, such as a
  coding agent. Add a version in parentheses only when the tool shows one.
- Cite a shared chat as Company. (Year, Month Day). *Title of chat* [Generative AI chat]. Tool or
  model name. Share URL. APA gives no form for a session without a share URL; cite the tool and give
  the relevant prompts and output in the text or an appendix.
- In the text, give the company and year, as in *(OpenAI, 2025)*.
- Describe each use where it occurs, such as in the method section or a table note, and cite the
  tool or chat there. An AI contribution statement may summarise all uses when the institution
  requests or permits one.
- Keep prompts out of the reference entry. Give a prompt that matters in the text or an appendix.
  When quoting output, state the prompt and quote the output with a citation.
- AI used only to find sources, or built into common software, needs no citation. Read and cite the
  sources it found.

Complete this branch when every reference entry is cited in the text and every cited work has a
matching entry, except a personal communication and the original work named in a secondary
citation. Every entry follows the APA form or a stated house adaptation. Report any source form that
remains unresolved.
