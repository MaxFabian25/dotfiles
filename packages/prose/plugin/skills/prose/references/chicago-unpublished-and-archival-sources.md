# Papers, contracts, reports, and manuscript collections

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns an unpublished, archival, contractual, report,
or manuscript source.

Record whether other readers can consult each source and under what access restrictions. A private
copy does not make a source available to other readers.

## Source types

| Source type | Required source elements | Other source elements and applicable branches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thesis or dissertation | Author, quoted title, kind of thesis or dissertation, academic institution, and date | Abstract label, database and identification number, URL, and locator |
| Unpublished manuscript | Author, quoted title or description, the words *unpublished manuscript*, and date when available | Whether others can consult it, locator, citation in text or a note, or the manuscript-collection rules |
| Conference paper or poster | Author, quoted title, presentation, meeting sponsorship, location, and date | Virtual status, URL, locator, and the rules for published proceedings, journal articles, or recorded lectures and talks |
| Working paper | Author, quoted title, formal series title and number, institution, and publication date | Month and day, URL, and locator |
| Report | Author, title, publisher, and date | Sufficient identifying information, report or series number, form, edition, URL, and locator |
| Private document | Person or parties named, kind of document, and date | Dates executed or proved, number, *in the author's possession* or *private collection*, manuscript-collection rules when held in a depository, permission limits, and locator |
| Item in a manuscript collection | Title and date of the item, series title when applicable, collection name, and depository | Item author, letter writer and recipient, communication form, box, container, folder, file, folio, page, image, identifier, depository location, database, and URL |
| Manuscript collection | Collection name, author or authors of the items, or depository | Series, dates, collection identifier, depository location, database, URL, and availability to other readers |

Use the most specific row. Treat a contract, will, or other document held in a depository as an
item in a manuscript collection. Do not treat it as a private document.

## Citation forms

| Citation form | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Full note | Begin with the specific item used. Follow it with the item date, series title when applicable, collection name, and depository. Add the specific page or other locator that supports the citation. |
| Shortened note | Retain the item author or letter writer, a distinctive item title or description, and the useful locator. Define any collection abbreviation before using it. |
| Bibliography | Begin with the author for a freestanding publication. For a manuscript collection, begin with the collection name, the author or authors of the items, or the depository. Include a specific item only when one item warrants an entry. |
| Author-date reference | Put the year after the author for a dated freestanding publication. An entry for a manuscript collection need not invent a year. Record individual item dates in the text. |
| Author-date text citation | Use the name or collection in the reference-list entry when one exists. Otherwise, give complete identifying details in text or a permitted note. Add the year and locator only when the source record and selected form require them. |

Apply the selected system's punctuation, name order, date position, short-title, and locator rules.
The source type controls the available fields.

## Papers, contracts, and reports

- Put a thesis or dissertation title in quotation marks, not italics.
- Record the kind of thesis or dissertation, academic institution, and date after the title.
- For an online work, add a stable URL. For a commercial database, record its name and supplied
  identifier.
- Add an abstract label only when the citation is to the abstract.
- For conference work, record whether it is a paper, poster, or other presentation. Record the
  meeting sponsorship, location or virtual status, and date.
- Cite an unpublished paper or poster in text or a note by default. Add a URL when one exists.
- Treat published proceedings as a chapter in a book or a journal article, according to their
  published form.
- For a working paper, preserve a formal series title and number from the source.
- In author-date, place any month and day after the publication details.
- Treat a freestanding report like a book while giving sufficient data on its author and publisher
  to identify it.
- When author and publisher data do not fit the usual pattern, give enough information to identify
  the document.

## Unpublished manuscripts and private contracts, wills, and such

- Keep a privately shared unpublished manuscript in text or a note by default.
- Put its specific title in quotation marks and identify its unpublished status.
- Do not add it to a bibliography unless governing policy and reader need justify the entry.
- Keep an unarchived contract, will, or comparable private document in text or a note by default.
- In place of the depository, write *in the author's possession* or *private collection*, as
  applicable. Omit the location.
- Do not invent a depository, collection, or locator, or claim that others can consult the
  source.
- Apply confidentiality, permission, and [Rights, permissions, and credit
  lines](references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines)
  limits before quoting private material.

## Items in manuscript collections

- Record the item title or description, item date, series when applicable, collection, and
  depository.
- Put a specific item title in quotation marks. Leave a generic item description unquoted.
- Preserve formal heading capitalisation. Lowercase a description supplied only for the citation.
- Begin a note citation of a letter with the writer and recipient. Name another communication form
  when it helps identification.
- Use one date style consistently in citations of manuscript collections.
- Record the series and file numbers and any other identifiers supplied by the depository.
- Distinguish a folio, page, image, box, container, folder, and file in the source record.
- Include the depository's location only when readers need it to distinguish the depository.
- For a collection consulted online, cite it like the physical collection and add the database or
  URL.
- When many items are cited, choose the first element of the bibliography or reference-list entry.
  Use the collection name, the author or authors of the items, or the depository.
- When only one item warrants an entry, that item may lead the entry.
- In author-date, do not add *n.d.* to a collection merely because the collection lacks one date.
- Put an individual item's date in the text when the entry for the collection omits it.
- For one undated item beginning with the writer's name, use *n.d.* under [Undated and forthcoming
  works](chicago-source-forms.md#undated-and-forthcoming-works) only when the selected form requires
  a date.
- Let proximity, abbreviations, and readers' convenience determine each shortened form.

Complete this branch when every cited source meets its source type's metadata, availability, and
citation-form requirements. Report any unresolved required field.
