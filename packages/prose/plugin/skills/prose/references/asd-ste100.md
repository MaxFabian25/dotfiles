# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

Select this branch only from an explicit user, project, contract, or governing
technical-documentation requirement for ASD-STE100 in original English prose. Internal readership,
English language, and a technical subject supply no selection authority.

Use the registered name **ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English**, Issue 9, January 2025. The
title page presents *Standard for technical documentation* as the subtitle, and the page footer
gives January 15, 2025. Use **Simplified Technical English (STE)** when a short form is useful.

ASD identifies STE as a controlled natural language and an international standard to write
technical documentation. The source contains **writing rules** in Part 1 and a **dictionary** in Part 2.

Use these source terms only for their source concepts: **approved word**, **approved meaning**, and
**word that is not approved**. Apply the same rule to **approved alternative**, **technical noun**,
and **technical verb**. Preserve
the part of speech and approved meaning of a general-language dictionary entry.

## Writing rules

- Use no more than 20 words in an instruction, warning, or caution. Use no more than 25 words in a
  descriptive sentence or note.
- Count a hyphenated word as one word. Count text inside parentheses as one word. Count quoted
  text as one word. In a vertical list, count the introduction and each item as separate sentences.
  Meet the word limit by splitting or rewriting the sentence.
- Do not use contractions or omit a noun, verb, subject, or article to meet a length limit. Split or
  rewrite the sentence.
- Use no more than six sentences in a descriptive paragraph. Split a longer paragraph at a genuine
  topic boundary.
- Use active voice in an instruction. In descriptive prose, use passive voice only when the agent
  is unknown.
- Use simple present for a current state, simple past for a completed action, and simple future for
  a future action.
- Use the perfect aspect only when completion before another event is material. Use
  the progressive aspect only when continuity at a stated time is material. In an evidence record,
  name the dates, events, or duration that make the complex form necessary. Do not use either aspect
  only to sound current or formal. The aspect permissions are a house departure. ASD-STE100 Rule
  3.2 approves only the simple tenses, the infinitive, the
  imperative, and the past participle.
- Use no more than three words in a multi-word noun. For a longer established technical noun, give the full term at first use. Then use a defined
  short form, add a preposition, or hyphenate words that form one unit.
- Use approved words only with their approved meanings. Use one technical noun for one item. Use a
  recurring technical term only as a part of speech that its subject field recognises.

## Adopted word rules

Entries that widen the dictionary's approved alternatives are house adaptations.

- Use *check* as a noun for a named procedure, including the form *do a check*. Do not use
  *check* as a verb. Use *make sure* for a required outcome, *measure* for a quantity, and
  *examine* for direct observation. Use *verify*, *inspect*, *test*, or *review* only with their
  established method meanings.
- Replace *assess* with *calculate* for deterministic arithmetic. Use *estimate* for an uncertain
  quantity or condition. Retain *assess* only when established professional usage names a judgement
  against stated criteria.
- Reserve *establish* for sufficient evidence of a proposition or an action that creates a formal
  state. Use *set* or *create* for configuration, *determine* for a calculation or decision, and
  *show* or *indicate* for weaker evidence.
- Replace a phrasal verb with one exact verb unless the phrasal verb is an established term or is
  clearer.
- Replace *appropriate*, *suitable*, *adequate*, and *relevant* with the applicable condition,
  threshold, or relationship. The dictionary approves *applicable* for *appropriate* and
  *suitable*, *sufficient* for *adequate*, and *related* for *relevant*. Use *sufficient* only
  when the minimum or condition is stated.
- Replace *provide* and *obtain* with the exact action. Use *let* for permission. Replace the verb
  *maintain* with *keep*, *hold*, *update*, *preserve*, *service*, or *repair*, as applicable.

## Adopted punctuation

Apply these house punctuation rules in prose that this branch governs:

- Use double quotation marks for a quotation and single marks for a quotation inside it.
- Put full stops and commas inside the closing quotation mark. Put them outside when exact governed
  text requires it.
- Do not use an em dash in original prose. Recast the sentence with a sentence break, a comma pair,
  parentheses, or a colon.
- Do not use a semicolon. Replace it with two sentences under Rule 8.1. This restriction overrides
  an APA punctuation option only within the ASD-STE100 branch.
- Write capital-letter abbreviations without full stops, including UK and US.
