# Chicago book sources

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns a book or book part. Use [Chicago source
forms](chicago-source-forms.md) for shared citation-form,
no-author, and locator mechanics.

## Source types

| Source type | Required source elements | Other source elements and applicable branches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Book | Author, editor, or organisation listed as author, full title, publisher, and year | Roles, edition, contributors, volumes, series, pre-1900 place, locator, and access data |
| Chapter or other book part | Author and title of the chapter or other contribution, book title, publisher, and year | Book editor or author, chapter or volume data, locator, access data, and part-specific rules |
| Edition or reprint | Author or editor, title, consulted-edition statement, publisher, and year | Original publication facts, edition relationship, consulted-edition locator, and medium |
| Multivolume work | Author or editor, set title, citation level, publisher, and applicable years | Volume count, volume or book data, contributors, series, and locator |
| Book in a series | Book author or editor and title, publisher, and year | Useful series title, number, designation, editor, and multivolume data |
| Electronic book or audiobook | Book metadata, access form, and needed access source | Reflowable format, stable locator, narrator, time data, and fixed medium |

## Elements to include when citing a book

- Take the author's or editor's name and the title from the title page when possible. Verify
  uncertain bibliographic terms against a reliable library catalogue.
- When an editor, compiler, or translator leads the entry, add the applicable abbreviated role.
  Keep it in full notes, bibliographies, and author-date references. Omit it from shortened notes
  and text citations.
- When a contributor follows an author, place the contributor after the title. Abbreviate the role
  in a note and spell it out in a bibliography or reference-list entry.
- Keep multiple contributor roles in the source's order. Include a foreword writer, illustrator,
  collaborator, or other contributor only when that role helps readers identify or assess the
  source. Label the role accurately.
- A first edition needs no edition statement. Put a later-edition label after the title and before
  any volume information.
- For a chapter or other contribution, put the part title before *in* and the book title.
  Quote a contribution title, but italicise a part that is itself a book-length work.
- Do not add a chapter's inclusive pages to a bibliography or reference list merely to complete a
  template. Keep the specific page reference in the full note or author-date text citation.
- When several contributions from one multiauthor book are cited in notes and bibliography, a
  bibliography may cross-refer from the parts to a full book entry. Do not use those component
  cross-references in author-date. List and cite the collection instead.
- For an untitled introduction, foreword, preface, or afterword, record the part description and
  author. Also record the book title and author plus any relevant editor or translator.
- In a note for published correspondence, identify sender, recipient, communication date, the
  collection, and the locator. In author-date, cite the collection's publication year and put the
  communication date in the prose.

## Edition, multivolume works, and series

- Distinguish an edition from a later printing, impression, or copyright renewal. Ignore the
  printing, impression, or renewal unless it identifies a materially different source.
- Cite the edition actually consulted. Add original publication facts only when they matter to the
  claim or source history. Identify which edition supplies the locator.
- In author-date, an original year may precede the consulted year in parentheses in the reference
  entry. Repeat that original year in square brackets before the consulted year in the text
  citation.
- For a whole multivolume set, record the total volume count and the set's year or year range. For
  an unfinished set, leave the range open after its first year.
- For one volume, record the volume number and title, the contributors responsible for that
  volume, and that volume's publication year. Record both collection and volume years when they
  differ and both are material.
- Use Arabic volume numbers. Render a volume and page as `volume:page` with no spaces and without
  *vol.* or *p.*. Retain *vol.* when a volume is cited without a page.
- Assign each general editor and volume editor to the title for which that person is responsible.
  If one volume occupies more than one physical book, record both the volume and book number.
- Include series data only when it improves discovery or identification. Set a series title in
  roman type without quotation marks. Use title case for English series and the applicable
  sentence case for non-English series.
- Put a series number after its title. Retain *vol.*, *no.*, a new-series label, or a comparable
  marker when needed to prevent ambiguity. Usually omit the series editor.
- Classify a finite named series cited collectively as a multivolume work. Italicise that collective
  title. Otherwise, cite the individual book and keep the series title in roman type.

## Facts of publication

- For a book published in or after 1900, record the publisher and year. Do not add a publication
  city by default.
- For a book published before 1900, normally record place and year. Add the publisher only when it
  is useful, and separate the place from that publisher with a colon.
- Use the publisher's name at the time of publication. Omit a leading *The*, corporate suffixes,
  and generic company wording that does not help identify the publisher. Retain *Press* when its
  omission would misidentify the organisation, including a university press.
- Preserve a publisher's meaningful punctuation and its source-language wording. Do not translate
  a non-English publisher name.
- For an independently issued work without an imprint, use a published-by-author statement. Omit
  a retail platform that only distributed the work.
- Prefer the identifiable imprint. Include a parent academic institution or joint imprint when
  that relationship helps readers assess the source.
- For a copublication, use the publisher most relevant to the citation's readers. Record both when
  needed to identify the edition. Omit a distributor unless the title-page evidence makes it part
  of the publication statement or it materially identifies the source.
- Record only the year for a book. Use the edition's year, not a later impression or renewed
  copyright year.
- For a book with no date, an estimated date, or forthcoming publication, apply [Undated and
  forthcoming works](chicago-source-forms.md#undated-and-forthcoming-works).

## Book-specific locating information

- In a bibliography or author-date reference, omit ordinary references to specific book pages and
  total page counts.

## Electronic books

- Do not label a fixed-layout ebook when it reliably matches the print edition. Name a reflowable
  application or format because pagination and layout may differ.
- For a reflowable book, prefer a chapter, section heading, or numbered paragraph to a device-only
  location. Use a page number only when it corresponds to a verified print edition.
- Put the electronic format after the locator in a note and at the end of a bibliography or
  reference entry.
- For an audiobook, record the narrator. Use a time stamp for a specific note. Give the total
  duration when a bibliography entry needs to identify the edition.
- For a book consulted on the web, apply [Link selection and
  behaviour](references-and-citations.md#link-selection-and-behaviour).
- For an older work available online, identify the underlying edition. Prefer an authoritative
  page scan with stable pages to an unattributed or reflowed transcription.
- For a book on fixed media, put the medium after the publication details and after any note
  locator.
