# Editorial source evidence

## Supporting editorial literature

The Apple guide supplies supporting editorial evidence for instructions about user interfaces,
inclusive wording, technical notation, and global-audience content. It governs Apple names and terminology only
when Apple is the applicable product authority. It supplies established usage, not authority for
the standards that it cites.

The Microsoft guide supplies supporting editorial evidence for technical, interface, global,
accessibility, and conversational content. It governs Microsoft names and terminology only when
Microsoft is the applicable product authority.

The Red Hat supplementary style guide governs Red Hat names and terminology only when Red Hat is the
applicable product authority. It supplements IBM Style, which Prose has not reviewed. The PDF states
no licence; its repository declares CC BY-SA 4.0, so Prose uses independently worded rules and
reproduces none of its text.

The Oxford guide supplies supporting editorial evidence for *Spelling*, *Inclusive communication*,
*Identity and personal characteristics*, *References*, *Capitalisation*, and *Numbers*. It also
supports *Punctuation*, *Names and titles*, *Highlighting/emphasising text*, and *Footnotes*. Its
scope is communications produced for the University of Oxford. Its stated audience excludes public
and external use. Professional writing
guides and dictionaries retain their authority. It supplies established usage, not general
authority.

No applicable reproduction licence was verified for the guide. The skill uses the guide's
terminology where necessary and otherwise states the rules independently. It reproduces no extended
passage.

## Clarity

Clarity supplies editorial guidance adapted into these independently worded rules:

- The [rules for voice samples](../editorial-practice.md#voice-samples) separate stylistic evidence from the
  factual material for a new piece.
- The [reasoning checks](../reader-core.md#reasoning-checks) test the claim and implied connection
  beneath a rhetorical form.
- [Authored prose](../authored-prose.md) uses focused questions to obtain missing author material,
  develops arguments within their evidence, supports comprehension on first hearing in talks and
  speeches, and checks whether an edit has weakened effective craft.
  [Editorial verification](../editorial-verification.md) owns the associated whole-text read-through.
- [Editorial practice](../editorial-practice.md) prioritises whole-piece review findings by their
  effect on the piece's purpose and its reader.

The questions about the author's material apply when the brief or a claim needs information that is missing.
This adaptation leaves interviews conditional, permits useful synthesis without personal
experience or novelty, and retains Prose's completion criteria. The craft guidance applies to
English and Danish as a local editorial adaptation. The source establishes neither Danish
language conventions nor comparative writing quality.
