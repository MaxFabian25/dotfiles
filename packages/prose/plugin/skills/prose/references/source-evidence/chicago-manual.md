# Chicago source evidence

## Chicago Manual of Style

Treat this manual as supporting editorial literature for general mechanisms. Treat it as the
direct style authority only when a user or governing source explicitly selects Chicago. Apply
that authority only to the selected editorial, quotation, or citation scope. It does not govern
APA, accessibility conformance, law, community naming, or the skill's global authority
hierarchy.

### Publication and editing

Sections 1.79, 1.99–1.100, and 1.121–1.124 support errata and corrections, retractions, the
version of record, notification of readers, and supplemental journal content. Sections 1.84–1.85
and 1.101–1.102 support accurate and updated metadata and abstracts and keywords that help
readers find and assess a work.

The skill uses sections 2.37–2.40 for cross-checking the manuscript and checking
cross-references. Sections 2.41, 2.45, 2.83–2.92, and 2.105–2.115 support preparing and cleaning
up a final manuscript for production. They also support removing comments, queries, tracked
changes, revision marks, and hidden text.

Sections 2.105–2.123 and 2.139–2.144 support proofreading, keeping a record of each proofreading
stage, and double-checking proofs. They also support proofing and testing electronic formats and
checking for accessibility with tools and human reviewers.

Sections 2.53–2.62 support developmental editing, line editing, copyediting, proofreading,
mechanical editing, substantive editing, and editorial discretion. Sections 2.54 and 2.74–2.78
support sensitivity and authenticity readings, author comments and queries, and editorial style
sheets. They also support the author's last opportunity to make substantive changes.

### Rights and table content

Sections 2.48–2.52, 3.30–3.38, 3.77–3.81, 4.79–4.81, 4.99–4.106, and 4.110–4.115 support rights
and permissions records, the scope of rights, and credit lines. They support the terms
*reprinted*, *adapted*, and *data from*. Sections 4.1–4.2 state that the chapter is not legal
advice. Its law is that of the United States. The skill therefore routes legal decisions to the
applicable legal authority.

Sections 3.48–3.50 and 3.82–3.87 support editing table content, including checks for internal
consistency, consistency across multiple tables, consistency with the surrounding text, totals,
note references, relevance, redundancy, and number ranges. They ask whether a lay reader or
nontechnical editor can make logical sense of a table.

Sections 3.37–3.38, 4.6, 4.51–4.52, and 4.76 support credits for AI-generated images and
disclosure of AI use. They distinguish expression created by AI from expression created by the
human using AI. They also address the author's copyright warranties and inadvertent reproduction
of copyrighted material. They establish no universal authorship, originality, copyright, or
policy rule. Current governing authority controls each decision.

### Grammar and mechanics

Sections 5.1–5.5 and 5.30–5.40 support pronoun antecedents, pronoun-antecedent agreement,
participial phrases, gerunds, and dangling participles or gerunds. Sections 5.110–5.148 support
agreement in person and number, placement of adverbs, and prepositional idiom. The skill also
uses sections 5.172–5.177, 5.200–5.201, 5.209, 5.212, and 5.237–5.252. They cover beginning a
sentence with a conjunction, omitting *that*, elliptical sentences, negation, expletives, and
parallel structure.

Sections 6.17, 6.41–6.42, and 6.46–6.47 cover commas in pairs, commas with dates and addresses,
and commas in personal and corporate names. Sections 6.53–6.58, 6.65, and 6.67 cover commas with
*such as* and *including*, explanatory alternatives, direct address, and elision. They also
cover colons and lowercase or capital letters after a colon. Sections 6.79–6.100 cover hyphens
and dashes. The skill adopts none of Chicago's 2-em or 3-em dash forms. Sections 6.102 and
6.105–6.109 cover parentheses, square brackets in translated text, square brackets for
parentheses within parentheses, and parentheses and brackets in mathematics and phonetics.

Sections 7.1–7.3, 7.23, and 7.27–7.28 support recommended dictionaries, variant spellings, and
joint versus separate possession. They also support possessive versus attributive forms for
groups and the possessive with a gerund. Sections 7.90–7.91, 7.95, and the introductory
paragraph of 7.96 support open, hyphenated, closed, permanent, and temporary compounds. They
also support compound modifiers, suspended hyphens, and the hyphenation guide. Sections 8.1–8.2
and 8.20–8.22 support proper nouns and adjectives and titles and offices. Sections 8.157–8.204
support sentence case, title case, and the listed categories under “Titles of Works.” The skill
adopts those categories only when the selected authority calls for them. It does not apply
Chicago title case globally.

Sections 9.1–9.8 support choosing between spelling numbers out and using numerals, consistency
and flexibility, and numbers beginning a sentence. The skill does not adopt Chicago's general
rule of spelling out zero through one hundred globally. Section 9.9 supports *millions,
billions, and so forth*. Sections 9.14–9.19, 9.35–9.42, and 9.67–9.69 support physical
quantities, units of measure, centuries, dates and times, noon and midnight, and Roman numerals.
Sections 9.28–9.54 cover numbered divisions in books and other documents, numbers with proper
names and titles, and addresses and thoroughfares. The live rules use only Chicago's guidance
for postal addresses unless a selected authority calls for another listed form. Section 9.55
supports plural numbers. Sections 9.62–9.66 cover inclusive numbers. The skill does not adopt
Chicago's abbreviated inclusive-number forms globally. The [British English
overlay](../english-core.md#british-english-spelling) and [authority
order](../../SKILL.md#authority-and-preservation) remain controlling rather than Chicago's US
dictionary and American spelling preference.

Sections 10.1–10.12 and 10.48–10.76 support acronyms, initialisms, contractions, periods,
spaces, capitals, and lowercase letters. Their supported abbreviation guidance includes spelling
out less familiar abbreviations at first occurrence and, when needed, in later major divisions;
articles before abbreviations; and specialised abbreviations and symbols, including SI units.
The skill retains its four-use house threshold outside an explicitly selected Chicago scope and
reproduces no abbreviation table.

### Source text and other languages

Sections 10.78 and 14.106 support the [source-text](../source-text.md) treatment of emojis in
source content. This includes retaining emojis and rendering each as a special character when
the corresponding Unicode character is available, treating an emoji as art when no corresponding
character exists, and using an in-line image with alternative text when precise historical
rendering is required. These rules do not change the local rule for original prose.

Sections 11.2–11.12 and 11.79–11.106 support Unicode, words and phrases from other languages,
and non-English proper nouns in an English context. They also support translations of terms from
other languages, language tags, transliteration, character sets for non-Latin alphabets,
diacritics, and right-to-left text. The skill reproduces no language table and leaves rendering
implementation to the output host.

Sections 11.13–11.19 and 11.78–11.119 support quotations from other languages, crediting and
adjusting the translation of a quoted passage, avoiding retranslation, and transliteration or
romanisation systems. Language and community authorities control individual forms. The skill
copies no romanisation table.

Sections 12.7–12.8, 12.18–12.21, and 12.70–12.74 support permissible changes to quotations,
logical and grammatical assimilation, interpolations and clarifications, *sic*, and identifying
added or original italics. They do not supply jurisdiction-specific reuse rights.

Sections 12.9–12.29 and 12.59–12.83 support choosing between run-in and block quotations, block
quotations, poetry extracts, ellipses, bracketed clarifications, and attributing quotations in
text. The skill confines Chicago's block convention (about 100 words or multiple paragraphs) to
an explicitly selected Chicago quotation scope. Its Chicago-style ellipsis of three spaced
periods and parenthetical source placement share that scope. Sections 12.39–12.58 support the
[creative and dialogue branch](../creative-and-dialogue-prose.md).

### Citations and research records

Sections 13.6–13.12 support [Link selection and
behaviour](../references-and-citations.md#link-selection-and-behaviour) and cover URLs and other
identifiers, including uniform resource locators (URLs), digital object identifiers (DOIs), and
permalinks. They also cover editing long URLs, library and other bibliographic databases, and
link-shortening services. Sections 13.13–13.17 support [Keeping track of your
research](../references-and-citations.md#keeping-track-of-your-research) and the date rules in
[Citation placement and style](../references-and-citations.md#citation-placement-and-style). They
cover citation management tools, double-checking data and citations, and backing up citations.
They also support citing the right version, access dates, revision dates, and preserving a
permanent record.

Sections 13.1–13.5 support the [Chicago scope and systems
branch](../chicago-scope-and-systems.md) and its choice between notes and bibliography and
author-date. The branch treats this manual as direct authority only when Chicago has been
selected and never displaces APA without an explicit requirement.

Sections 13.18–13.101 support [Chicago notes and
bibliography](../chicago-notes-and-bibliography.md): Basic Format, Notes and Bibliography:
Examples and Variations, Notes, Bibliographies, Author’s Name, and Title of Work. Sections
13.102–13.128 support [Chicago author-date](../chicago-author-date.md): Basic Format,
Author-Date: Examples and Variations, Author-Date Reference Lists, and Author-Date Text
Citations.

Section 13.81 was checked against the eighteenth edition on 6 September 2026, at printed pages
822–823. When the author or editor is unknown, it normally places the title first in notes and
bibliography entries and ignores an initial article for alphabetisation. It permits limited
grouping under *Anonymous* or *Anon.* and retains an explicit attribution to Anonymous. The
[no-author rules](../chicago-source-forms.md#no-author-and-unlisted-forms) adopt the title-first
default and preserve explicit source attribution.

### Published, web, and unpublished sources

Sections 14.1–14.102 support [Chicago source forms](../chicago-source-forms.md), [Book
sources](../chicago-book-sources.md), [Periodical sources](../chicago-periodical-sources.md),
and [Review sources](../chicago-review-sources.md). Covered sources include books, parts of a
book, editions, multivolume works, series, and electronic books. They also include journal and
magazine articles, news articles, and reviews. These sections support full notes, shortened
notes, bibliography entries, reference list entries, and text citations. The modules reproduce
none of the manual's examples.

Sections 14.103–14.111 support [Websites, blogs, social media, interviews, and personal
communications](../chicago-web-and-personal-sources.md). They support page or site titles and
descriptions, owners or sponsors, dates, time stamps, URLs, permanent publicly available
archives, correspondents, subjects or threads, lists or forums, and host sites. For blog posts,
the supported fields are the author, post title, blog title, larger publication title, date, and
URL; for social media posts, the name, handle, original post text, platform, date, and URL; for
interviews, the person interviewed, interviewer, place or date, and location of any transcript
or recording; and for personal communications, the person, communication, and date. These
sections also support treating private content, including direct messages and posts on private
accounts, as personal communication; omitting an email address or the like belonging to an
individual; and retaining quoted wording and emoji.

Section 14.112 supports [Citing AI-generated content](../chicago-ai-sources.md), including
disclosure of tool use, citation of specific content where it occurs, and identification of the
tool and its publisher or developer. It also supports recording available version numbers, the
generation date, and prompts; linking to publicly archived copies; and acknowledging edited or
adapted AI-generated text. [Source text](../source-text.md) separately governs alterations, and
[Rights, permissions, and credit
lines](../references-and-citations.md#rights-permissions-and-credit-lines) separately governs
rights. Section 14.112 does not establish AI-generated content as an authority or determine its
rights status.

Sections 14.113–14.129 support [Papers, contracts, reports, and manuscript
collections](../chicago-unpublished-and-archival-sources.md), including theses, dissertations,
unpublished manuscripts, conference work, working papers, reports, private documents, items in
manuscript collections, and manuscript collections. They also support recording whether other
readers can consult the material. For items in manuscript collections, the supported fields are
the item's title and date, series title when applicable, collection name, depository, and folio,
page, series, and file numbers. These sections support notes beginning with the specific item.
Bibliography entries may begin with the collection name, the author or authors of the items, or
the depository.

### Specialist and community sources

Sections 14.130–14.132 support [Special types of references](../chicago-specialist-sources.md).
They support resource and entry titles, the author of a signed entry, edition, publication,
update, or access date, and URL. They also support citing well-known dictionaries and
encyclopaedias in notes under the resource title, citing an entry rather than a volume or page,
using *under* instead of *s.v.*, listing other reference works like books, and citing substantial
signed entries by author.

Sections 14.133–14.135 support recording the artist, title or description, date of creation or
completion, medium, location of the work, museum accession number, and URL. They also support
separating a citation from a caption or credit line.

Sections 14.138–14.154 support scriptural references, other sacred works, and classical works.
They support the cited version or translator, book, chapter, verse, or other identifying
numbers, and edition, editor, or translator when relevant.

Section 14.157 supports databases and datasets. Its citation guidance supports recording the
displayed title, source type, repository, publication or access date, years to which data apply,
accession number, and DOI URL or other URL.

Section 14.158 supports the names of patent creators, description, year of filing, patent
number, and issue date. It also supports a URL.

Section 14.159 supports the organisation, title, designation, edition, approval or reaffirmation
date, cited clause, and URL for a standard.

Section 14.160 supports citations taken from secondary sources. It supports distinguishing the
unavailable original from the secondary source actually consulted. The module routes specialist
form to its governing authority and copies no abbreviation or numbering table. Sections
14.155–14.156 supply no rules in this module.

Sections 14.136–14.137 support [Indigenous sources](../chicago-indigenous-sources.md), with
distinct handling for secondary sources and information acquired directly from an Indigenous
Elder or other Indigenous Knowledge Keeper. The supported records include the name, nation or
community, information or teaching, date of interaction, and optional treaty territory and where
they live. These sections also support confirming whether the information is suitable for
publication. They support additional protocol, additional permission, and any preference for a
Traditional Name or legal name. The module gives these confirmations priority over generic
Chicago formatting. It infers no permission and reproduces no citation template.

### Multimedia and legal sources

Sections 14.161–14.169 support [Multimedia](../chicago-multimedia-sources.md), including the
person, group, or other entity responsible for creating or compiling the content; titles or
descriptions; publishers or other entities that make the content available; recording or
publication dates; media; and URLs. They support recordings, live performances, videos,
podcasts, and apps. Track, scene, episode, time stamp, and other usable locators must identify
the version consulted.

Sections 14.170–14.207 support [Legal and public
documents](../chicago-legal-and-public-sources.md), including recommended stylebooks and legal
and public documents online. They cover cases and court decisions, constitutions, and
legislative and executive documents. They also cover applicable citation guides for Canada, the
United Kingdom, and international entities. They distinguish print sources, online versions
authenticated by a government entity, official versions, exact copies of official versions, and
commercial databases. The applicable citation guide or requirement controls citation form.

### Indexes and additional sources

Sections 15.1–15.8, 15.15–15.23, 15.29–15.34, 15.109–15.129, and 15.131–15.132 support indexes
made with human input, embedded indexes, and standard keyword vocabularies. They also support
indexing terms, main headings, full entries, locators, direct links to the text, *see* and *see
also* references, the role of the indexer, and the distinction between a concordance and an
index. The skill leaves index ordering, alphabetisation, layout, and typography to the governing
host.

Chicago supplies no copied legal abbreviations, forms, or tables. The skill also uses the
“Bibliography of Additional Resources,” printed pages 1029–1050, to identify additional sources.
Treat the listed works as starting points. Verify each source against the task and its governing
authority before relying on it.

### Future adaptations and reproduction

A later rule derived from this manual must add its exact section scope here in the same change.

The edition reserves all rights. No reproduction licence is included or verified. Retain source
metadata and section designations. Use independently worded adaptations. Do not copy the

specialist abbreviations and legal forms.
