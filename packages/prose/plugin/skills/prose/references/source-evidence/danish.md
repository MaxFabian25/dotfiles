# Danish source evidence

## Danish

Use *Retskrivningsordbogen* 5.1 (`AUTH-DSN-RO-5-1`) for Danish spelling, inflection, and
punctuation. Its rule examples write clock times with a full stop, as in *kl. 23.30*. The [Danish
prose module](../danish-core.md) routes consultation. It does not reproduce dictionary entries.
The registered term *startkomma* occurs in § 49-50, *Ledsætninger* (`AUTH-DSN-RO-COMMAS`).

### Danish supporting literature

These sources support the independently worded Danish rules for sentence construction, LIX
reporting, bureaucratic register, the conventional designation *forståelighed*, and reader testing.
The historical public-sector guides and the handbook establish a Danish plain-language tradition.
The think-aloud guide supports reader testing. The *Den Danske Ordbog* entries for *milliard*,
*billion*, and *trillion* support the Danish large-number names. They do not replace the official
orthographic authority, and a numerical target remains limited to the source's own readers and
method.

### Danish institutional guidance

The borger.dk and lifeindenmark.dk guide controls those publishers' content. The digital-inclusion
principle supports public-service communication that helps citizens understand and act. The
sundhed.dk guide applies to its two handbooks, and the courts' language policy applies to court
communication. Treat each as scoped institutional guidance, not as a universal rule for Danish
prose.

### Danish practitioner guidance

The Omatskrive.dk pages support practical choices for professional, technical, report, email, and
letter prose. They also identify jargon and inflated wording that merit review. Treat them as
practitioner guidance below controlling project, publisher, official, and orthographic authority.
The LIX page can support a declared counting method, but neither it nor a LIX score demonstrates
actual reader comprehension or supplies a universal target.

### APA in Danish prose

APA publishes no Danish edition. The Danish APA terms follow the guides of CBS Library, SDU Library,
and Roskilde University Library on the points each addresses. Where they differ, the defaults take
the form that most Danish university guides use or the form closest to APA's English structure.
Danish guides differ on the comma before *&*, page ranges, works in press, secondary-source wording,
date forms, and retrieval wording, so an institution's own guide controls those choices.
