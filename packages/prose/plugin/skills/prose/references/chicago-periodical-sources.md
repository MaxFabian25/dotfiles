# Chicago periodical sources

Apply this branch when a Chicago citation concerns a journal, magazine, newspaper, or news-site
article. Use [Chicago source forms](chicago-source-forms.md) for shared citation-form,
no-author, and locator mechanics.

## Source types

| Source type | Required source elements | Other source elements and applicable branches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journal article | Article author and title, journal title, year, and available issue data | Page range or article identifier, locator, extra date, access data, and publication status |
| Magazine article | Named author, article and magazine titles, and full issue date | Department, locator, access data, and publication-title substitute for an unsigned article |
| Newspaper or news-site article | Named author, headline, publication title, and full date | Edition, section, locator, posted time, access data, retained copy, and unsigned treatment |

## Periodicals

- Put an article title in quotation marks and the periodical title in italics. Do not insert *in*
  between them.
- Treat a periodical with accessible volume data as a journal. When no volume or issue can be
  found, use magazine form.
- For a journal, record volume, issue number, and year. Add month or season only when volume or
  issue data are missing or when the extra date distinguishes the issue.
- In notes and text citations, give only the location cited. In a bibliography or reference list,
  keep the full journal article range, except when a continuous-publication article identifier
  replaces it.
- For an online article, apply [Link selection and
  behaviour](references-and-citations.md#link-selection-and-behaviour). Add an access date only
  when governing instructions require it.
- For a journal article published ahead of print, use its posted publication date and available volume,
  issue, and page data. Use forthcoming for an accepted article that has not reached that stage.
  For an unaccepted manuscript, apply [Unpublished
  manuscripts](chicago-unpublished-and-archival-sources.md#unpublished-manuscripts-and-private-contracts-wills-and-such).
- Before finalising a citation to a forthcoming article or an article published ahead of print, check
  whether final publication details now exist. Recheck every quotation and locator against the final
  version.
- For a preprint, replace journal issue data with the preprint designation, server, posted date,
  and stable identifier. Do not describe an accepted journal proof as a preprint merely because a
  publisher does.
- Distinguish a special issue, which uses the journal's ordinary numbering, from a separately
  numbered supplement. Record the issue or supplement editor and title when the whole unit or its
  theme matters.
- Record instalments separately unless one combined entry makes their relationship clearer.
  Cite the version actually consulted when an article appears in more than one publication.
- Treat a translated or edited article like other contributor-bearing works. Do not create a
  separate bibliography entry merely for an abstract. Identify an online supplement through the
  parent article unless it is an independently published dataset or media work.
- For a magazine, use the complete issue date rather than volume and issue numbers. A note may give
  a specific page number, but a bibliography need not give the article's scattered inclusive pages.
- A magazine department or regular feature is in roman type without quotation marks. Use the
  magazine title as the lead element for an unsigned article or department when needed.
- For a newspaper or news site, always record month, day, and year. Add edition, section, page,
  column, or posted time only when it identifies the cited version or place.
- Convert a cited news headline to title case within Chicago scope. When a headline has a sentence
  break, use a colon if the combined title remains clear.
- Preserve enough place or edition information to distinguish publications with similar names.
  Set a news-service name in roman type. Treat a weekend magazine or supplement as a magazine.
- News and magazine articles may remain in text or notes without bibliography entries when the
  recorded bibliography policy permits. Retain a copy when a cited news page can change.

Complete this branch when each periodical source has one type, verified facts, and complete fields.
